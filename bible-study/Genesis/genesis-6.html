Genesis

Chapter Six

King James Version
And it came to pass, when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them, 2That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose.

3And the LORD said, My spirit shall not always strive with man, for that he also is flesh: yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years.

4There were giants in the earth in those days; and also after that, when the sons of God came in unto the daughters of men, and they bare children to them, the same became mighty men which were of old, men of renown.

5And God saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. 6And it repented the LORD that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him at his heart. 7And the LORD said, I will destroy man whom I have created from the face of the earth; both man, and beast, and the creeping thing, and the fowls of the air; for it repenteth me that I have made them. 8But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.

9These are the generations of Noah: Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with God. 10And Noah begat three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

11The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence. 12And God looked upon the earth, and, behold, it was corrupt; for all flesh had corrupted his way upon the earth. 13And God said unto Noah, The end of all flesh is come before me; for the earth is filled with violence through them; and, behold, I will destroy them with the earth.

14Make thee an ark of gopher wood; rooms shalt thou make in the ark, and shalt pitch it within and without with pitch. 15And this is the fashion which thou shalt make it of: The length of the ark shall be three hundred cubits, the breadth of it fifty cubits, and the height of it thirty cubits. 16A window shalt thou make to the ark, and in a cubit shalt thou finish it above; and the door of the ark shalt thou set in the side thereof; with lower, second, and third stories shalt thou make it.

17And, behold, I, even I, do bring a flood of waters upon the earth, to destroy all flesh, wherein is the breath of life, from under heaven; and every thing that is in the earth shall die. 18But with thee will I establish my covenant; and thou shalt come into the ark, thou, and thy sons, and thy wife, and thy sons' wives with thee.

19And of every living thing of all flesh, two of every sort shalt thou bring into the ark, to keep them alive with thee; they shall be male and female. 20Of fowls after their kind, and of cattle after their kind, of every creeping thing of the earth after his kind, two of every sort shall come unto thee, to keep them alive. 21And take thou unto thee of all food that is eaten, and thou shalt gather it to thee; and it shall be for food for thee, and for them.

22Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he.

Commentary: The Two Lines Converge, and One Man Finds Grace

The Sons of God: Not Fallen Angels Giants and Men of Renown A Sign of the End Time A Hundred and Twenty Years Noah Found Grace One Door in the Ark
Every doctrine traced from Genesis 3 through 5 — the two seeds, the line that went out from the Presence of the Lord, the line that called on His name — comes to a head in this chapter's opening verses. Two lines that have run side by side since Cain and Abel now intermarry, and Brother Branham read verses 1 through 4 as the record of exactly that convergence, not as a stranger and more exotic event.

The Sons of God: Not Fallen Angels

Verse 2's “sons of God” has a long history of being read as fallen angels taking human wives. Brother Branham addressed that interpretation directly, and rejected it, on two grounds: it would make Satan a creator, which Scripture nowhere allows, and the Bible gives no indication that angels have sex at all.
“
“Many of you know that theologians believe, that, ‘These fallen angels, that had fell by Lucifer's lie, took themselves wives…pressed themselves into flesh, and took wives.’ I…just a little don't think that just makes reasonings with the Scripture…Before a fallen angel could make hisself a man, he would first have to be a creator. And there is only one Creator, and that's God. So that would wash it out.”
— William Branham, “The Conflict Between God and Satan” (62-0531, paras. 120–121)
“
“Many of them says here that that was the fallen Angels…But to show that it is wrong, it's absolutely wrong, the Bible doesn't say anywhere about Angels being either men or women…there is never a—a woman spoke of as being an Angel, in the Bible, never.”
— William Branham, “The Flashing Red Light of the Sign of His Coming” (63-0623E, para. 60)
In place of that reading, he identified the “sons of God” as the line of Seth — the same righteous line traced through Genesis 5 — and the “daughters of men” specifically as the line of Cain.
“
“I think, ‘These sons of God was the sons of Seth, saw the daughters of the Cainites, the Cains, that they were fair.’ Because, ‘The sons of Seth came, was the son of Adam, who was the son of God.’ But, ‘The…daughters of Cain, who was the son of Satan.’”
— William Branham, “The Conflict Between God and Satan” (62-0531, para. 123)
“
“Genesis 6…Did you notice, when the sons of God…which was the sons of Seth that come from Adam, and from Adam was God, son of God, son of Seth, then the other side come from Cain, which was believing Satan's lie, sons of Satan.”
— William Branham, “Putting on the Whole Armor of God” (62-0607, para. 88)
On this reading, the intermarrying of verses 1 and 2 is the two seed-lines of Genesis 3:15 finally crossing back into each other — the very thing that had kept them distinct since Cain went out from the Presence of the Lord now beginning to dissolve.

Giants and Men of Renown

Verse 4's “giants…mighty men…men of renown” picks up the same civilization Brother Branham had already traced running through Cain's line in the previous chapters — builders, inventors, and now, on his reading, a physically imposing generation as well.
“
“When we come over through the lineage of Cain…they went off into the land of Nod, Cain did, and there married. Took his wife and went in there, and later man become like giants…there come a great civilization through that lineage of—of Cain back there…a great worker of wood…great discoveries of education, and great going on of science…a sign that the end time was near.”
— William Branham, “The Ark” (55-0522, paras. 51, 53)
He set this civilization's outward greatness directly against the humility of the line that produced Noah, treating the contrast as the point of the whole passage rather than an incidental detail.
“
“These smart, educated Cainite women…married these humble peasant boys of Seth. And from there, became ‘giants’ and were ‘renown.’ We find out that they become great scientists, great workers of wood…But the sons of God, that stayed humble, stayed away from such things as that, and relied upon God, knowing that they were going to inherit all things in the end.”
— William Branham, “The Conflict Between God and Satan” (62-0531, paras. 133–134)
Type & Shadow — Renown and Humility
Verse 4 hands history's spotlight to the giants, the mighty men, the ones of renown — and the very next verses hand history's judgment to the world they built. The line that stayed humble, unrecorded, and unremarkable is the one still standing when the chapter ends. Fame and the favor of God are not shown here running in the same direction.

A Sign of the End Time

Brother Branham preached this chapter as directly relevant to his own hearers, not as ancient history, taking Jesus' own words in Matthew 24 as the authorization to compare the two ages point for point: the multiplying of the earth's population, and a widespread increase in outward female beauty and self-display, both read as recurring signs rather than one-time antediluvian curiosities.
“
“The very One Who wrote Genesis 6, told us in Matthew 24, to know when the time was nigh, to go back to Genesis 6 and compare it…There never has been a time on the earth…that there ever was such a—a wholesale beauty among women…that's a flash of red light that the time is at hand.”
— William Branham, “The Flashing Red Light of the Sign of His Coming” (63-0623E, paras. 44, 49, 52)

A Hundred and Twenty Years

Verse 3's warning follows immediately on the heels of the intermarrying in verses 1 and 2, and Brother Branham read the sequence as cause and consequence rather than coincidence.
“
“When people begin to multiply upon the earth, and the women got fair, it was that hour, it was at that time, that: ‘the LORD said, My spirit shall not always strive with man’…You see the clutch? Do you see the hand of God? ‘My Spirit!’ The daughters of men were fair; and they took unto them wives. And He said, then, ‘My Spirit will not always strive with man. I'll allot his days.’”
— William Branham, “The Flashing Red Light of the Sign of His Coming” (63-0623E, para. 57)

Noah Found Grace

Verse 8 turns the whole chapter on a single sentence, and Brother Branham preached an entire message under this chapter's title built around it: in a world God is about to judge, one man is found, and the reason given is not his own effort but grace shown to him.
“
“I want to speak this morning…upon: The Ark; what the ark meant, and what the ark foreshadowed, and the types of the ark. For, the ark perfectly, and everything in the Old Testament, typed Christ.”
— William Branham, “The Ark” (55-0522, para. 45)

One Door in the Ark

Verses 14 through 16 give the ark's dimensions and specify a single door in its side. Brother Branham read that detail as anything but incidental — the same pattern he found running through the whole of Scripture, from the shepherd sleeping across his sheepfold's one entrance to Christ's own words in John 10.
“
“Noah, in the Old Testament, stood in the door of the ark…He stood in the door and preached repentance and righteousness to the people that laughed at him. And in that same door that he stood in, no man could come into the ark except by that one door. Wasn't but one door in the ark…There's only one Door to the Church of the living God, and Jesus is that Door!”
— William Branham, “Revelation, Chapter Four, Part I” (60-1231, paras. 92–93)
Type & Shadow — The One Door
A vessel large enough to save every kind of living thing, and specified down to its window and its rooms, is given only one way in. Whoever entered the ark entered exactly the same way as everyone else standing beside them, rich or poor, righteous family or last-minute convert — through that single door, before it was shut. Salvation from the flood was not a matter of merit measured at the door; it was a matter of being on the right side of it when it closed.
