Genesis

Chapter Thirty-Four

King James Version
And Dinah the daughter of Leah, which she bare unto Jacob, went out to see the daughters of the land. 2And when Shechem the son of Hamor the Hivite, prince of the country, saw her, he took her, and lay with her, and defiled her. 3And his soul clave unto Dinah the daughter of Jacob, and he loved the damsel, and spake kindly unto the damsel. 4And Shechem spake unto his father Hamor, saying, Get me this damsel to wife.

5And Jacob heard that he had defiled Dinah his daughter: now his sons were with his cattle in the field: and Jacob held his peace until they were come. 6And Hamor the father of Shechem went out unto Jacob to commune with him. 7And the sons of Jacob came out of the field when they heard it: and the men were grieved, and they were very wroth, because he had wrought folly in Israel in lying with Jacob's daughter; which thing ought not to be done.

8And Hamor communed with them, saying, The soul of my son Shechem longeth for your daughter: I pray you give her him to wife. 9And make ye marriages with us, and give your daughters unto us, and take our daughters unto you. 10And ye shall dwell with us: and the land shall be before you; dwell and trade ye therein, and get you possessions therein. 11And Shechem said unto her father and unto her brethren, Let me find grace in your eyes, and what ye shall say unto me I will give. 12Ask me never so much dowry and gift, and I will give according as ye shall say unto me: but give me the damsel to wife.

13And the sons of Jacob answered Shechem and Hamor his father deceitfully, and said, because he had defiled Dinah their sister: 14And they said unto them, We cannot do this thing, to give our sister to one that is uncircumcised; for that were a reproach unto us: 15But in this will we consent unto you: If ye will be as we be, that every male of you be circumcised; 16Then will we give our daughters unto you, and we will take your daughters to us, and we will dwell with you, and we will become one people. 17But if ye will not hearken unto us, to be circumcised; then will we take our daughter, and we will be gone.

18And their words pleased Hamor, and Shechem Hamor's son. 19And the young man deferred not to do the thing, because he had delight in Jacob's daughter: and he was more honourable than all the house of his father. 20And Hamor and Shechem his son came unto the gate of their city, and communed with the men of their city, saying, 21These men are peaceable with us; therefore let them dwell in the land, and trade therein; for the land, behold, it is large enough for them; let us take their daughters to us for wives, and let us give them our daughters. 22Only herein will the men consent unto us for to dwell with us, to be one people, if every male among us be circumcised, as they are circumcised. 23Shall not their cattle and their substance and every beast of theirs be ours? only let us consent unto them, and they will dwell with us. 24And unto Hamor and unto Shechem his son hearkened all that went out of the gate of his city; and every male was circumcised, all that went out of the gate of his city.

25And it came to pass on the third day, when they were sore, that two of the sons of Jacob, Simeon and Levi, Dinah's brethren, took each man his sword, and came upon the city boldly, and slew all the males. 26And they slew Hamor and Shechem his son with the edge of the sword, and took Dinah out of Shechem's house, and went out. 27The sons of Jacob came upon the slain, and spoiled the city, because they had defiled their sister. 28They took their sheep, and their oxen, and their asses, and that which was in the city, and that which was in the field, 29And all their wealth, and all their little ones, and their wives took they captive, and spoiled even all that was in the house.

30And Jacob said to Simeon and Levi, Ye have troubled me to make me to stink among the inhabitants of the land, among the Canaanites and the Perizzites: and I being few in number, they shall gather themselves together against me, and slay me; and I shall be destroyed, I and my house. 31And they said, Should he deal with our sister as with an harlot?

Commentary: A Wrong Answered by a Greater Wrong

Dinah Defiled A Sign Turned Into a Weapon Ye Have Troubled Me
Genesis 34 is one of the darkest chapters in the patriarchal narratives, and no sourced Branham commentary specifically addressing this chapter's events turned up anywhere in the corpus searched for this study — not the assault on Dinah, not Hamor's proposal, not Simeon and Levi's deceitful use of circumcision as a condition for marriage, not the massacre of the city, and not Jacob's rebuke at the chapter's close. Rather than force a reading onto a chapter the available preaching material never addresses, this study presents the text plainly, on its own terms, with the same rigor for accuracy the rest of this commentary holds to.

Dinah Defiled

The chapter opens with an act of violence against Jacob's daughter, described by Scripture in the plainest terms: Shechem "took her, and lay with her, and defiled her." What follows — his professed love for her, and his father's proposal of full intermarriage between the two peoples — does not undo or excuse the violence that opens the account; Scripture itself never treats Shechem's later affection as a resolution to what he had already done. Dinah's own voice is entirely absent from the chapter; she is spoken about, bargained over, and fought over by men on every side, but never once quoted.

A Sign Turned Into a Weapon

Simeon and Levi's proposal — requiring the men of Shechem to be circumcised before any marriage could take place — takes the very sign of God's covenant with Abraham, already studied at length in this commentary's reading of Genesis 17, and uses it as a tool of deception and, ultimately, of mass killing while the men of the city were incapacitated by the wound. Genesis states their intent as deceit outright, in verse 13, before the plan is even carried out. The brothers' own justification, given to their father in verse 31 — "Should he deal with our sister as with an harlot?" — states a real grievance without settling whether the answer they gave it was itself righteous; Jacob's own response in verse 30 treats their action as folly and danger to the whole family, not as vindication.

Ye Have Troubled Me

Jacob's rebuke closes the chapter on a note of practical fear rather than moral judgment as such — his stated concern is that his sons have endangered the family among the surrounding peoples, "few in number" as they are. Scripture's own final verdict on this episode comes only much later, on Jacob's deathbed in Genesis 49, where he curses Simeon and Levi's anger rather than praising it: "Cursed be their anger, for it was fierce; and their wrath, for it was cruel." No sourced Branham commentary connecting Genesis 34 to that later deathbed judgment turned up in the corpus searched for this study, but the connection is Scripture's own, made explicit by Jacob himself decades later.

Scripture Cross-References

Genesis 17:10–14 — the covenant sign of circumcision, already studied, here turned to deceitful use  •  Genesis 49:5–7 — Jacob's deathbed curse on Simeon and Levi's anger, Scripture's own later judgment on this chapter  •  Deuteronomy 22:28–29 — later Mosaic law addressing a comparable violation  •  2 Samuel 13:1–22 — the later violation of Tamar, another daughter of Israel whose own voice Scripture likewise records only briefly  •  Genesis 35:5 — "the terror of God was upon the cities that were round about them," following directly from this chapter's events  •  1 Chronicles 4:24–43, Numbers 3:5–10 — the later, divergent fates of Simeon (scattered, absorbed into Judah) and Levi (set apart for the priesthood) after this shared act
