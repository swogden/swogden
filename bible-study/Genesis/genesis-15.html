Genesis

Chapter Fifteen

King James Version
After these things the word of the LORD came unto Abram in a vision, saying, Fear not, Abram: I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward. 2And Abram said, LORD God, what wilt thou give me, seeing I go childless, and the steward of my house is this Eliezer of Damascus? 3And Abram said, Behold, to me thou hast given no seed: and, lo, one born in my house is mine heir. 4And, behold, the word of the LORD came unto him, saying, This shall not be thine heir; but he that shall come forth out of thine own bowels shall be thine heir.

5And he brought him forth abroad, and said, Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them: and he said unto him, So shall thy seed be. 6And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.

7And he said unto him, I am the LORD that brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees, to give thee this land to inherit it. 8And he said, Lord GOD, whereby shall I know that I shall inherit it? 9And he said unto him, Take me an heifer of three years old, and a she goat of three years old, and a ram of three years old, and a turtledove, and a young pigeon. 10And he took unto him all these, and divided them in the midst, and laid each piece one against another: but the birds divided he not. 11And when the fowls came down upon the carcases, Abram drove them away.

12And when the sun was going down, a deep sleep fell upon Abram; and, lo, an horror of great darkness fell upon him. 13And he said unto Abram, Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, and shall serve them; and they shall afflict them four hundred years; 14And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance. 15And thou shalt go to thy fathers in peace; thou shalt be buried in a good old age. 16But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.

17And it came to pass, that, when the sun went down, and it was dark, behold a smoking furnace, and a burning lamp that passed between those pieces. 18In the same day the LORD made a covenant with Abram, saying, Unto thy seed have I given this land, from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates: 19The Kenites, and the Kenizzites, and the Kadmonites, 20And the Hittites, and the Perizzites, and the Rephaims, 21And the Amorites, and the Canaanites, and the Girgashites, and the Jebusites.

Commentary: Counted for Righteousness, Sealed by an Oath

I Am Thy Shield Counted for Righteousness The Sign Abram Asked For A Horror of Great Darkness Darkness, Furnace, and a Burning Lamp
Genesis 15 is where Abram's faith is named for what it is, in the verse the rest of Scripture keeps returning to, and where God confirms His promise the only way Brother Branham said an oath could ever be confirmed — by Himself alone.

I Am Thy Shield

Verses 1 through 4 open with a vision and a complaint: God tells Abram not to fear, and Abram, still childless after years of promise, points out that his own household servant Eliezer stands to inherit everything instead. God's answer is immediate and specific — not a servant, but a son from Abram's own body. Brother Branham read this whole exchange from Hebrews 6, where the writer treats it as the classic example of God confirming a promise by His own oath, since He had no one greater to swear by.

Counted for Righteousness

Verses 5 and 6 give Scripture its first statement of justification by faith, centuries before Paul builds an entire doctrine on it in Romans and Galatians. God takes Abram outside and tells him to count the stars, if he can, and promises his seed will match them. Abram's response is not a plan, a condition, or a bargain — simply belief, and that belief itself is what God counts as righteousness.
“
“He never said, 'Now, Lord, I'll tell you what I'll do. I'll do a certain-certain thing.' He just believed the Lord, and it was counted to him for righteousness, 'cause God said so. He had swore by it, that He would do it.”
— William Branham, “He Swore by Himself” (54-1212, para. 117)
He was careful to note that this faith did not stand alone, unaccompanied by any outward mark; God still gave Abraham a sign.
“
“Abraham believed God, that's right, and it was imputed to him for righteousness. But God give him a sign; circumcised him, as a sign, a seal.”
— William Branham, “He Swore by Himself” (54-1212, para. 122)

The Sign Abram Asked For

Verses 7 through 11 record a second, different question — not "will You?" but "how will I know?" — and God's answer is the strange ceremony that gives the chapter its name: a heifer, a goat, and a ram, each three years old, cut in half and laid in two rows, with a turtledove and a young pigeon left whole beside them. Brother Branham gave particular attention to why every animal was specified at three years old.
“
“Take the heifers, the goats, and everything, must be three years old. The three different dispensations; fatherhood, sonship, Holy Spirit…Only one God; three dispensations. Not three Gods; three dispensations of the same God!”
— William Branham, “He Swore by Himself” (54-1212, para. 144)
He also drew a distinction between the animals that were divided and the two birds that were not, tying the undivided birds to a covenant he believed carries through every dispensation without a break.
“
“The turtledove and pigeon, the reason it wasn't cut apart, because a turtledove and pigeon represents Divine healing. The covenant was broke from grace, from law to grace, but the covenant of healing lasts always, from all the way through, there's no cutting it apart at all.”
— William Branham, “Abraham” (55-0608, para. 107)

A Horror of Great Darkness

Verses 12 through 16 turn suddenly ominous: a deep sleep, a horror of great darkness, and a prophecy that Abram's descendants would be strangers and slaves in a foreign land for four hundred years before God judged their oppressors and brought them out with great wealth. Brother Branham read this as God foretelling the entire span of Israel's Egyptian bondage centuries in advance, exact in its terms even while Abram himself would die in peace long before any of it happened.
“
“God foretelling, unto Abraham, that way down in his great-great-great-grandchildren's would sojourn in a strange land, four hundred years, would be afflicted. Designated a time, exactly, how long it would be.”
— William Branham, “He Swore by Himself” (54-1212, para. 134)

Darkness, Furnace, and a Burning Lamp

Verse 17 is the chapter's most striking image: after the horror of darkness, a smoking furnace and a burning lamp pass alone between the divided pieces, with no mention of Abram walking through them at all. Brother Branham read the three stages of that vision — darkness, furnace, and light — as a picture of death, of hell, and finally of Christ Himself.
“
“First thing, was a horrible darkness, death; then, a smoking furnace, hell; but beyond that, went a little, white Light. I want you to notice. God was showing Abraham Calvary, what He was going to confirm, this covenant between Abraham and Abraham's seed, forever.”
— William Branham, “The Unconditional Covenant That God Made With the People” (54-0814, para. 84)
“
“So death comes first. Then hell was the next, the smoking furnace. Then he saw a burning Lamp, a Light…And this Lamp went between each one of those pieces, making a covenant, that God, someday through the promised seed would raise up the Lord Jesus Christ to confirm the covenant with all the peoples of the earth.”
— William Branham, “Jehovah-Jireh” (56-1209E, para. 33)
Type & Shadow — A Covenant God Walks Alone
In the ancient custom this ceremony reflects, both parties to a covenant would walk together between the divided pieces, each accepting the animals' fate as the penalty for breaking the agreement. Here, Abram is asleep. Only the furnace and the light pass through — God alone. Brother Branham's reading makes the point explicit: this covenant carries no condition Abram could fail to keep, because Abram was never a party required to walk it. God bound only Himself.

The Land Given, Border to Border

Verses 18 through 21 close the chapter with the covenant's territorial terms spelled out by name — from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates, and ten peoples already living on that ground. No sourced Branham commentary on this specific list of nations turned up in the corpus searched for this study; several of these names reappear in the fuller Table of Nations material covered in chapter 10, and this commentary does not go beyond noting that connection.

Scripture Cross-References

Hebrews 6:13–18 — God's oath by Himself, since He could swear by no greater  •  Romans 4:1–5 — Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness  •  Galatians 3:6–9 — they which are of faith are the children of Abraham  •  Exodus 12:40–41 — the sojourning of the children of Israel, fulfilling this chapter's four hundred years  •  Jeremiah 34:18–19 — passing between the parts of a covenant sacrifice, the same ancient custom  •  Genesis 10:15–19 — the Canaanite peoples named again in this chapter's land grant
