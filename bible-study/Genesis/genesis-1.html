Genesis

Chapter One

King James Version
In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. 2And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. 3And God said, Let there be light: and there was light. 4And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness. 5And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.

6And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters. 7And God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament: and it was so. 8And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day.

9And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so. 10And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that it was good. 11And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so. 12And the earth brought forth grass, and herb yielding seed after his kind, and the tree yielding fruit, whose seed was in itself, after his kind: and God saw that it was good. 13And the evening and the morning were the third day.

14And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years: 15And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so. 16And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also. 17And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth, 18And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good. 19And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

20And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven. 21And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that it was good. 22And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth. 23And the evening and the morning were the fifth day.

24And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so. 25And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

26And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth. 27So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them. 28And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth. 29And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat. 30And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat: and it was so. 31And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

Commentary: The Architecture of Genesis 1

The Godhead Twofold Creation of Man Dominion & the Spoken Word Ruin & Restoration The Seed Principle Six Days / Six Thousand Years
Every doctrine in this Book has its root system somewhere in this chapter. Before there is a Law, a Tabernacle, a Bride, or a Kingdom, there is this: God speaking, and what He speaks coming to pass. That is the pattern the rest of Scripture never departs from — the spoken Word of God, made manifest. Brother Branham built an entire ministry on the principle he found sitting right here in verse one, and returned to this chapter, by name, dozens of times across eighteen years of preaching. What follows draws that material together: not just three supporting quotes, but the doctrinal weight the Message placed on almost every verse of this chapter.

Order and Pattern: What Comes First, What Comes Last

Notice the shape of the creation week itself — what comes first and what comes last.
“
“If you notice, in creation, the first thing that was created, of course, was light. You come on down through the creation, the last thing was created was what? A man. And the woman was made after man. All right, the first…Last thing that was created, of God's creation, is mankind.”
— William Branham, “Questions and Answers on Genesis” (53-0729, para. 16)
Light opens the week; man closes it. Nothing in between is an accident of sequence — the plants that feed him, the sun that governs his days, the creatures he is given dominion over, all take their place before he ever draws breath. Man is the last thing spoken because he is the reason everything else was spoken.

The Godhead Unfolding: “Let Us”

That order reaches its height in verse 26, when the Voice that has been speaking creation into being turns and speaks to Itself: “Let Us make man in our image, after our likeness.” Not a command outward this time, but a counsel within the Godhead, and it is the verse Brother Branham leaned on hardest when explaining how the one God of Genesis 1:1 could, later in this same chapter, refer to Himself as “Us.”
“
“'Let Us' … He's talking to Someone, He was speaking to another Being … He manifested or unfolded Himself a little more, to make a trinity of Himself by Father, Son, and Holy Spirit … and here was God, unfolded Himself now, down into ‘let Us make man,’ which was His son, an offspring from Him, ‘man in Our Own image,’ he was a supernatural being.”
— William Branham, “Questions and Answers on Genesis” (53-0729, para. 44)
This same pairing of verses, Genesis 1:26–27 read against Hebrews 1:1–3, is how Brother Branham tied the oneness of God directly to the image man was made in — the Son as “the express image of his person,” and man as the image of that image.
“
“I want you to turn…to the 1st chapter of Hebrews, first…and then Genesis 1:26 and 7, to tie that together…'God, who at sundry times…spake in times past unto the fathers by the prophets, hath in this last days spoken unto us…by his Son…who being in the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person…' And now over in Genesis, the 1st chapter…'And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness.'”
— William Branham, “Oneness” (62-0211, para. 6–7)

Two Creations, Two Men: Genesis 1:26 and Genesis 2:7

No verse in this chapter carries more doctrinal freight in the Message than the relationship between verse 26 and the forming of Adam in the next chapter. Brother Branham was asked about it directly, and treated it as two distinct creative acts, not one narrated twice.
“
“What connection does this Genesis 1:26 have with Genesis 2:7? God created two men. And which was the man…how does it connect up in the Scripture? … in Genesis 1:26…God said, ‘Let Us.’ Now, ‘let Us,’ … He's talking to Someone, He was speaking to another Being. ‘Let Us make man in Our Own image after Our likeness, and let them have dominion over the cattles of the field.’”
— William Branham, “Questions and Answers on Genesis” (53-0729, para. 14)
The man of verse 26 is not yet two people. Brother Branham was specific that this was a single spirit-being, at once male and female in one person, un-separated, with no dust, no rib, no garden mentioned yet.
“
“What was Eve? Her and Adam were the same. They were called Adam. They were spirit. When He formed man, the first time, He made him in His Own image. And God is a Spirit. ‘In the image of God created He him, male and female created He them.’ … Then God formed man out of the dust of the earth and put this dual spirit into him, and that was the first Adam.”
— William Branham, “Jehovah-Jireh 1” (64-0402, para. 118)
That one dual spirit, not two spirits side by side, is what gets a body of dust in the next chapter, and is only later divided into two persons when Eve is separated out of Adam. Brother Branham returned to that same distinction, in the same terms, as late as 1963.
“
“Genesis 1 to Genesis 2 is absolutely a different thing. The 1st chapter of Genesis says, ‘In the beginning God created the heavens and earth,’ period. How He did it, and when He did it, that's up to Him…Then, ‘the world was without form, and void,’ then we start off from there, for the creation.”
— William Branham, “The Way of a True Prophet” (63-0119, para. 57)
However a reader lands on the mechanics of it, the doctrinal point is what Brother Branham kept coming back to: the man of Genesis 1:26 is not yet the man of the Garden. Genesis 2 will pick that thread up, and this study will pick it back up there.

Dominion and the Spoken Word: Man as “Amateur God”

What follows verse 26 is not a passive creature but a delegated authority. Verse 28 hands the newly made man dominion over everything that swims, flies, and walks, and that dominion worked by the same spoken-word pattern as everything else in the chapter.
“
“The Voice of God out there…The voice of man, rather, could speak and say…call the cattle this way, call the sheep over in this pasture, call the fishes to this water. See, he had dominion, everything obeyed him.”
— William Branham, “Questions and Answers on Genesis” (53-0729, para. 45)
Brother Branham pushed this further than most commentators are willing to: that dominion made the man of Genesis 1 a kind of god over his own domain, not by nature, but by delegation from the Father who made him.
“
“God made man to be the predominate over all the earth, he had dominion. And the earth was man's dominion…he was god over the earth. He could speak, and it would be so.”
— William Branham, “Adoption 1” (60-0515E, para. 100)
“Amateur god” is Brother Branham's own term for it: not self-existent, entirely dependent on the Father, but genuinely exercising the Father's authority over the earth by the same means the Father used to make it — the spoken word.

The Ruin Before the Remaking: “Without Form, and Void”

Verse 2 is where Brother Branham drew a firm line against pressing the chapter for a scientific age of the earth. “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth” is one statement, complete in itself; the six days that follow, beginning from “without form, and void,” are a separate, later work of ordering what already existed.
“
“Genesis 1, He said, ‘In the beginning God created the heavens and earth.’ Period! How long He done it, I don't know, nobody else does. God did that. That's period. That's the end of the sentence. ‘And,’ when He begin to use it, ‘the world without form, and void, and water was upon the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the water.’”
— William Branham, “Influence” (63-1130B, para. 105)
That distinction let him hold two things at once that many readers assume must conflict: an ancient, undated “beginning,” and a literal, recent six-day ordering of the earth for man. Whatever a reader concludes about the age of the earth, the chapter's own grammar gives room for both.

The Spoken Word Is the Original Seed

Nowhere did Brother Branham build more directly on this chapter than in the 1962 message that took its title from it. Reading straight through the six days, he isolated the one phrase that repeats through every day of creation: “after his kind.”
“
“Now I want to begin this morning, with my text, to use this: The Spoken Word Is the Original Seed…Now if you'll notice, God said, ‘Let it bring forth of it, in his kind.’ Whatever it was, it must be brought forth of its kind.”
— William Branham, “The Spoken Word Is the Original Seed” (62-0318, para. 29–30)
Every herb, every tree, every creature in this chapter reproduces strictly “after his kind,” because it was spoken into being as a seed carrying that kind within it. That principle, first laid down here on days three, five, and six, becomes the foundation of an entire Message doctrine of the Word: a promise spoken by God is a seed that can only, ever, bring forth exactly what was spoken, never a hybrid, never a substitute.

Answering the Critics: The Firmament Before the Sun

Brother Branham returned more than once to a specific criticism of this chapter: that Moses had the order of creation backward, putting the firmament ahead of the sun. He treated the later confirmation of that order as vindication, not embarrassment.
“
“Critics and scientific world once laughed and said, ‘Didn't God make an awful mistake when He wrote the Bible? … there's no such a thing as any light outside of sunlight, that He made the firmament before He made the sun.’ … science looked down with the spirit of meekness and consideration, and dug into it, and proved that God was right.”
— William Branham, “My Angel Shall Go Before Thee” (53-0216, para. 13)
Type & Shadow — Six Days, Six Thousand Years
Brother Branham read the creation week itself as a prophetic shadow of world history: six days of labor followed by a seventh of rest, mapped onto six thousand years of human history followed by the Millennium.
“
“As God labored six thousand years, we know that a thousand years…'One day in Heaven is a thousand years on earth,' Second Peter…And God built the world in six thousand years. And the Church labored against sin now for six thousand years. And the seventh thousand, God went to rest, and rested the seventh; and now a type of the Millennium.”
— William Branham, “The Mark of the Beast” (54-0513, para. 34)
Whatever a reader concludes about the six days as history, the point Moses is making stands untouched, and it is the point every thread above keeps circling back to: God's Word is the one thing in this chapter that never fails to produce exactly what it said.
