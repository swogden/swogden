Genesis

Chapter Thirty-Eight

King James Version
And it came to pass at that time, that Judah went down from his brethren, and turned in to a certain Adullamite, whose name was Hirah. 2And Judah saw there a daughter of a certain Canaanite, whose name was Shuah; and he took her, and went in unto her. 3And she conceived, and bare a son; and he called his name Er. 4And she conceived again, and bare a son; and she called his name Onan. 5And she yet again conceived, and bare a son; and called his name Shelah: and he was at Chezib, when she bare him.

6And Judah took a wife for Er his firstborn, whose name was Tamar. 7And Er, Judah's firstborn, was wicked in the sight of the LORD; and the LORD slew him. 8And Judah said unto Onan, Go in unto thy brother's wife, and marry her, and raise up seed to thy brother. 9And Onan knew that the seed should not be his; and it came to pass, when he went in unto his brother's wife, that he spilled it on the ground, lest that he should give seed to his brother. 10And the thing which he did displeased the LORD: wherefore he slew him also.

11Then said Judah to Tamar his daughter in law, Remain a widow at thy father's house, till Shelah my son be grown: for he said, Lest peradventure he die also, as his brethren did. And Tamar went and dwelt in her father's house.

12And in process of time the daughter of Shuah Judah's wife died; and Judah was comforted, and went up unto his sheepshearers to Timnath, he and his friend Hirah the Adullamite. 13And it was told Tamar, saying, Behold thy father in law goeth up to Timnath to shear his sheep. 14And she put her widow's garments off from her, and covered her with a vail, and wrapped herself, and sat in an open place, which is by the way to Timnath; for she saw that Shelah was grown, and she was not given unto him to wife.

15When Judah saw her, he thought her to be an harlot; because she had covered her face. 16And he turned unto her by the way, and said, Go to, I pray thee, let me come in unto thee; (for he knew not that she was his daughter in law.) And she said, What wilt thou give me, that thou mayest come in unto me? 17And he said, I will send thee a kid from the flock. And she said, Wilt thou give me a pledge, till thou send it? 18And he said, What pledge shall I give thee? And she said, Thy signet, and thy bracelets, and thy staff that is in thine hand. And he gave it her, and came in unto her, and she conceived by him. 19And she arose, and went away, and laid by her vail from her, and put on the garments of her widowhood.

20And Judah sent the kid by the hand of his friend the Adullamite, to receive his pledge from the woman's hand: but he found her not. 21Then he asked the men of that place, saying, Where is the harlot, that was openly by the way side? And they said, There was no harlot in this place. 22And he returned to Judah, and said, I cannot find her; and also the men of the place said, that there was no harlot in this place. 23And Judah said, Let her take it to her, lest we be shamed: behold, I sent this kid, and thou hast not found her.

24And it came to pass about three months after, that it was told Judah, saying, Tamar thy daughter in law hath played the harlot; and also, behold, she is with child by whoredom. And Judah said, Bring her forth, and let her be burnt. 25When she was brought forth, she sent to her father in law, saying, By the man, whose these are, am I with child: and she said, Discern, I pray thee, whose are these, the signet, and bracelets, and staff. 26And Judah acknowledged them, and said, She hath been more righteous than I; because that I gave her not to Shelah my son. And he knew her again no more.

27And it came to pass in the time of her travail, that, behold, twins were in her womb. 28And it came to pass, when she travailed, that the one put out his hand: and the midwife took and bound upon his hand a scarlet thread, saying, This came out first. 29And it came to pass, as he drew back his hand, that, behold, his brother came out: and she said, How hast thou broken forth? this breach be upon thee: therefore his name was called Pharez. 30And afterward came out his brother, that had the scarlet thread upon his hand: and his name was called Zarah.

Commentary: A Chapter Interrupting Its Own Story

Judah's Descent Two Sons the Lord Slew Tamar's Righteousness The Scarlet Thread
Genesis 38 breaks into the Joseph narrative — sandwiched between Joseph's sale into Egypt in chapter 37 and his arrival at Potiphar's house in chapter 39 — to tell an entirely separate story about the brother who proposed selling him. No sourced Branham commentary specifically addressing this chapter's events turned up anywhere in the corpus searched for this study: not Judah's departure from his brothers, not the deaths of Er and Onan, not Tamar's deception at the gate of Enaim, and not the birth of the twins Pharez and Zarah. As with Genesis 34, this study presents the chapter plainly, on Scripture's own terms, rather than supply commentary that was not found.

Judah's Descent

The chapter opens by noting, without comment, that Judah "went down from his brethren" to live among Canaanites, married a Canaanite woman, and fathered three sons by her. Coming immediately after Judah's proposal in the previous chapter to sell Joseph rather than kill him outright, the placement itself is suggestive: Scripture moves directly from Judah's role in that betrayal to an account of his own household unraveling among people outside the covenant line. The text draws no explicit moral connection between the two chapters, and this study does not supply one where Scripture itself is silent; the sequence is simply left to stand as Genesis arranges it.

Two Sons the Lord Slew

Er and Onan's deaths are recorded in nearly identical language — each "wicked in the sight of the LORD," each one the LORD Himself "slew." Er's specific offense is never named; Onan's is stated plainly as his refusal to give his brother's widow an heir while still taking the position of husband, a practice later codified in the Mosaic law of levirate marriage (Deuteronomy 25:5–6). Judah's own instruction to Onan in verse 8, and his withholding of Shelah from Tamar afterward in verse 11, frame the family's failure to honor that obligation to Tamar as the real hinge of the chapter, well before her own actions in verses 13 and following.

Tamar's Righteousness

Tamar's deception of Judah at the roadside is recorded without editorial praise or condemnation from the narrator; Scripture simply states what she did and lets Judah's own verdict in verse 26 stand as the chapter's judgment on it: "She hath been more righteous than I; because that I gave her not to Shelah my son." Judah's admission is directed at his own failure to fulfill a duty owed to her under the family's own custom, not at the particulars of how she exposed that failure. The signet, bracelets, and staff Tamar demanded as a pledge were not incidental objects; a signet in particular functioned as a personal seal, identifying its owner as surely as a name, which is precisely how it serves as evidence in verse 25.

The Scarlet Thread

The midwife's mark on the first hand to emerge, and the reversal that follows when Pharez is born ahead of his brother after all, closes the chapter on the same note the whole book of Genesis has repeated since Cain and Abel: the firstborn's natural claim does not settle who carries the promise forward. No sourced Branham commentary was found tying this particular reversal to that larger pattern, but the pattern itself is Scripture's own, and Pharez's line is the one Ruth 4:18–22 and Matthew 1:3 trace forward to David and to Christ — meaning this chapter, for all its silence in the corpus searched for this study, sits directly in the genealogical line the New Testament itself draws from Judah to the Messiah.

Scripture Cross-References

Deuteronomy 25:5–6 — the law of levirate marriage, the obligation Onan refused  •  Genesis 25:22–26 — Jacob and Esau, an earlier reversal of the firstborn's natural claim  •  Ruth 4:18–22 — the genealogy from Pharez down to David  •  Matthew 1:3 — Pharez (Phares) named in Christ's own genealogy, with Tamar named alongside him  •  Genesis 37:26–27 — Judah's proposal to sell Joseph, immediately preceding this chapter  •  Genesis 49:8–10 — Jacob's later blessing on Judah, naming the scepter and lawgiver that would not depart from his line
