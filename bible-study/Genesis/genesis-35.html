Genesis

Chapter Thirty-Five

King James Version
And God said unto Jacob, Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there: and make there an altar unto God, that appeared unto thee when thou fleddest from the face of Esau thy brother. 2Then Jacob said unto his household, and to all that were with him, Put away the strange gods that are among you, and be clean, and change your garments: 3And let us arise, and go up to Bethel; and I will make there an altar unto God, who answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me in the way which I went. 4And they gave unto Jacob all the strange gods which were in their hand, and all their earrings which were in their ears; and Jacob hid them under the oak which was by Shechem. 5And they journeyed: and the terror of God was upon the cities that were round about them, and they did not pursue after the sons of Jacob.

6So Jacob came to Luz, which is in the land of Canaan, that is, Bethel, he and all the people that were with him. 7And he built there an altar, and called the place Elbethel: because there God appeared unto him, when he fled from the face of his brother. 8But Deborah Rebekah's nurse died, and she was buried beneath Bethel under an oak: and the name of it was called Allonbachuth.

9And God appeared unto Jacob again, when he came out of Padanaram, and blessed him. 10And God said unto him, Thy name is Jacob: thy name shall not be called any more Jacob, but Israel shall be thy name: and he called his name Israel. 11And God said unto him, I am God Almighty: be fruitful and multiply; a nation and a company of nations shall be of thee, and kings shall come out of thy loins; 12And the land which I gave Abraham and Isaac, to thee I will give it, and to thy seed after thee will I give the land. 13And God went up from him in the place where he talked with him. 14And Jacob set up a pillar in the place where he talked with him, even a pillar of stone: and he poured a drink offering thereon, and he poured oil thereon. 15And Jacob called the name of the place where God spake with him, Bethel.

16And they journeyed from Bethel; and there was but a little way to come to Ephrath: and Rachel travailed, and she had hard labour. 17And it came to pass, when she was in hard labour, that the midwife said unto her, Fear not; thou shalt have this son also. 18And it came to pass, as her soul was in departing, (for she died) that she called his name Benoni: but his father called him Benjamin. 19And Rachel died, and was buried in the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem. 20And Jacob set a pillar upon her grave: that is the pillar of Rachel's grave unto this day. 21And Israel journeyed, and spread his tent beyond the tower of Edar.

22And it came to pass, when Israel dwelt in that land, that Reuben went and lay with Bilhah his father's concubine: and Israel heard it. Now the sons of Jacob were twelve: 23The sons of Leah; Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, and Simeon, and Levi, and Judah, and Issachar, and Zebulun: 24The sons of Rachel; Joseph, and Benjamin: 25And the sons of Bilhah, Rachel's handmaid; Dan, and Naphtali: 26And the sons of Zilpah, Leah's handmaid; Gad, and Asher: these are the sons of Jacob, which were born to him in Padanaram.

27And Jacob came unto Isaac his father unto Mamre, unto the city of Arbah, which is Hebron, where Abraham and Isaac sojourned. 28And the days of Isaac were an hundred and fourscore years. 29And Isaac gave up the ghost, and died, and was gathered unto his people, being old and full of days: and his sons Esau and Jacob buried him.

Commentary: A Vow Kept, a Family Buried, a Promise Sealed

Put Away the Strange Gods A Vow Fulfilled Decades Later Isaac Died, and He Slept With Abraham
Genesis 35 closes the whole Jacob narrative that began at Bethel in chapter 28 — the vow made there is finally paid, the name change confirmed there is repeated and made official, and the chapter ends with the death of Isaac, folding this generation into the same burial pattern already traced in this commentary's study of Genesis 23 and 25. Between these bookends the chapter also carries some of its heaviest losses: Deborah, Rachel, and, in the very next generation, a fresh betrayal within Jacob's own household.

Put Away the Strange Gods

Verses 1 through 4 record Jacob commanding his entire household to surrender every foreign idol before going up to Bethel — the same household background already identified in this commentary's study of Genesis 29 and 31, where Laban's line was traced back to the idol-worship of Ur and the tower of Babel, and where Rachel herself was shown to have carried her father's household gods out of Padanaram. No sourced Branham commentary specifically addressing this purification scene, or Jacob's decision to bury the idols rather than simply discard them, turned up in the corpus searched for this study. Read against the background already established, though, this moment is the household's own reckoning with exactly the idolatry Brother Branham traced to its source two chapters earlier — not a new problem introduced here, but an old one finally addressed.

A Vow Fulfilled Decades Later

Verses 9 through 15 confirm, in God's own second appearance, the very promise and the very name change Jacob had already received once, alone, at night, twenty years earlier at this same location and once more at Peniel. No sourced Branham commentary specifically addressing this second Bethel appearance, or the significance of God confirming the name Israel a second time, turned up in the corpus searched for this study. What Scripture itself shows plainly, without needing a preacher's comment to draw it out, is that Jacob's vow in Genesis 28:20–22 — conditional, bargaining language spoken by a frightened young fugitive — is finally paid here by an older man returning as promised, tithe and altar both, exactly as he said he would.

Rachel's Death and a Household in Mourning

Rachel dies giving birth to the son she names with her last breath Ben-oni, "son of my sorrow," renamed by his father Benjamin, "son of my right hand." No sourced Branham commentary specifically addressing Rachel's death, the naming of Benjamin, or Reuben's sin with Bilhah in verse 22 turned up in the corpus searched for this study. Scripture itself states Reuben's act and Israel's hearing of it without further comment in this chapter; its consequence, like Simeon and Levi's violence in the previous chapter, is left to surface only later, in Jacob's own deathbed judgment in Genesis 49.
Type & Shadow — Isaac Died, and He Slept With Abraham
Isaac's death and burial in verses 28 and 29 is the next link in a burial chain Brother Branham traced repeatedly and consistently across several sermons: Job, then Sarah, then Abraham, then Isaac, each one gathered to the same ground in Canaan rather than wherever they happened to die, as a quiet testimony to a shared resurrection faith centered on the promised land rather than on Egypt or any other country. He returned to this exact sequence more than once, in nearly identical language each time.

Isaac died, and he slept with Abraham. Isaac begot Jacob. Jacob died, way down in Egypt, he said, 'Don't bury me down here. Swear that you won't bury me here! Take my bones and bury them up there with my father.' Why? Why? That's not written, brother, it's in between the line.”
— William Branham, “Israel and the Church 2” (53-0326, paras. 63–64)

“Abraham bought a place, and he…not…and he buried Sarah. And he begot Isaac. Isaac was buried by the side of his father and mother. Isaac begot Jacob; Jacob died down in Israel…in Egypt, brought back into Palestine. And then, Joseph also died down there. And they brought Joseph's bones back, 'cause he said, 'Make…' ha…”
— William Branham, “Questions and Answers 1” (64-0823M, para. 263)
Isaac's death in this chapter is the middle link of that chain, quietly confirming what the earlier study of Genesis 23 already established: the pattern was not accidental, and Brother Branham considered it significant enough to repeat, essentially unchanged, across sermons a decade apart.

Scripture Cross-References

Genesis 28:18–22 — Jacob's original vow at Bethel, finally fulfilled in this chapter  •  Genesis 31:19, 34 — Rachel's earlier theft of household idols, background to verses 2–4  •  Genesis 32:28 — the first giving of the name Israel, confirmed again here in verse 10  •  Genesis 23:19–20; 25:9–10 — the burial of Sarah and Abraham, continuing the same pattern completed here for Isaac  •  Genesis 49:3–4 — Jacob's deathbed judgment on Reuben for the sin recorded in verse 22  •  Ruth 4:11, Micah 5:2 — Ephrath and Bethlehem, the site of Rachel's death, later tied to the birthplace of David and of Christ
