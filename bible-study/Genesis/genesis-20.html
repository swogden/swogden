Genesis

Chapter Twenty

King James Version
And Abraham journeyed from thence toward the south country, and dwelled between Kadesh and Shur, and sojourned in Gerar. 2And Abraham said of Sarah his wife, She is my sister: and Abimelech king of Gerar sent, and took Sarah.

3But God came to Abimelech in a dream by night, and said to him, Behold, thou art but a dead man, for the woman which thou hast taken; for she is a man's wife. 4But Abimelech had not come near her: and he said, LORD, wilt thou slay also a righteous nation? 5Said he not unto me, She is my sister? and she, even she herself said, He is my brother: in the integrity of my heart and innocency of my hands have I done this. 6And God said unto him in a dream, Yea, I know that thou didst this in the integrity of thy heart; for I also withheld thee from sinning against me: therefore suffered I thee not to touch her. 7Now therefore restore the man his wife; for he is a prophet, and he shall pray for thee, and thou shalt live: and if thou restore her not, know thou that thou shalt surely die, thou, and all that are thine.

8Therefore Abimelech rose early in the morning, and called all his servants, and told all these things in their ears: and the men were sore afraid. 9Then Abimelech called Abraham, and said unto him, What hast thou done unto us? and what have I offended thee, that thou hast brought on me and on my kingdom a great sin? thou hast done deeds unto me that ought not to be done. 10And Abimelech said unto Abraham, What sawest thou, that thou hast done this thing?

11And Abraham said, Because I thought, Surely the fear of God is not in this place; and they will slay me for my wife's sake. 12And yet indeed she is my sister; she is the daughter of my father, but not the daughter of my mother; and she became my wife. 13And it came to pass, when God caused me to wander from my father's house, that I said unto her, This is thy kindness which thou shalt shew unto me; at every place whither we shall come, say of me, He is my brother.

14And Abimelech took sheep, and oxen, and menservants, and womenservants, and gave them unto Abraham, and restored him Sarah his wife. 15And Abimelech said, Behold, my land is before thee: dwell where it pleaseth thee. 16And unto Sarah he said, Behold, I have given thy brother a thousand pieces of silver: behold, he is to thee a covering of the eyes, unto all that are with thee, and with all: thou art rightly reproved.

17So Abraham prayed unto God: and God healed Abimelech, and his wife, and his maidservants; and they bare children. 18For the LORD had fast closed up all the wombs of the house of Abimelech, because of Sarah Abraham's wife.

Study: A Sin Prevented, Not Just Forgiven

This is the same fear playing out a second time — the same half-truth, the same request of Sarah, even the same basic setup as Egypt back in chapter 12. And verse 13 reveals something the earlier account never mentioned: this wasn't a one-time panic. "At every place whither we shall come, say of me, He is my brother" was a standing policy between them from the very start of their travels, not something Abraham improvised under pressure this one time.
What's genuinely different here is how God handles it. In Egypt, the wrong was already underway before anything was said — plagues fell on Pharaoh's house after the fact. This time, God intervenes first, in a dream, before Abimelech has touched her at all.
“
“He dozed off to sleep and God appeared to him, said, ‘You're just as good as a dead man.’... He said, ‘Well, Lord, You know the integrity of my heart.’ ...Said, ‘I know the integrity of your heart, that's the reason I kept you from sinning against Me. But if you don't restore her back, you're a dead man.’ ...Said, ‘Her husband is My prophet.’”
— William Branham, “Abraham” (55-0608)
That's a remarkable exchange to notice closely: God doesn't just forgive Abimelech after the fact, He states plainly that his heart really was innocent, and that the reason nothing happened is that God Himself held him back. And this is the first time in the entire Bible the word "prophet" is used of anyone — applied here to Abraham, in the very chapter where he's caught in a repeated, deliberate deception.
There's a stranger detail folded into this same telling: Abimelech's attraction to Sarah at what the text elsewhere calls her advanced age is explained as a real, visible renewal — tied to the same promise of Isaac's coming birth that chapters 17 and 18 already put in motion.
“
“He turned Abraham and Sarah, both, back to about twenty-five years old, like He's going to do everybody one of these days that's His children... God had turned her back to a beautiful young woman again, like He is going to do all the seed of Abraham someday.”
— William Branham, “Abraham” (55-0608)
Whatever a reader makes of that detail, Abimelech's confrontation of Abraham the next morning is entirely justified, and Abraham, for once, actually answers rather than staying silent the way he did in Egypt. His explanation reveals the deception was never a flat lie to begin with — just never the whole truth, either.
“
“Said, ‘Abraham, why did you do this?’ Said, ‘Why, she is my sister, as I told you she was. But,’ said, ‘she become my wife.’”
— William Branham, “The Uncertain Sound” (60-1218)
Sarah, it turns out, really was Abraham's half-sister — his father's daughter by another mother — which makes the whole arrangement technically true and genuinely misleading at the same time. Abimelech pays Abraham handsomely, restores Sarah, and offers him the run of the land, and the chapter closes on an oddly gracious note: Abraham prays for the very household his own fear had put at risk, and God heals them.
It's worth flagging plainly, without dwelling on it yet, that this exact pattern doesn't end with Abraham. His son will do something remarkably similar with his own wife, in almost identical circumstances, a few chapters from now — whatever this fear is, it apparently isn't cured in one generation.
