Genesis

Chapter Twenty-Three

King James Version
And Sarah was an hundred and seven and twenty years old: these were the years of the life of Sarah. 2And Sarah died in Kirjatharba; the same is Hebron in the land of Canaan: and Abraham came to mourn for Sarah, and to weep for her.

3And Abraham stood up from before his dead, and spake unto the sons of Heth, saying, 4I am a stranger and a sojourner with you: give me a possession of a buryingplace with you, that I may bury my dead out of my sight. 5And the children of Heth answered Abraham, saying unto him, 6Hear us, my lord: thou art a mighty prince among us: in the choice of our sepulchres bury thy dead; none of us shall withhold from thee his sepulchre, but that thou mayest bury thy dead.

7And Abraham stood up, and bowed himself to the people of the land, even to the children of Heth. 8And he communed with them, saying, If it be your mind that I should bury my dead out of my sight; hear me, and intreat for me to Ephron the son of Zohar, 9That he may give me the cave of Machpelah, which he hath, which is in the end of his field; for as much money as it is worth he shall give it me for a possession of a buryingplace amongst you.

10And Ephron dwelt among the children of Heth: and Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham in the audience of the children of Heth, even of all that went in at the gate of his city, saying, 11Nay, my lord, hear me: the field give I thee, and the cave that is therein, I give it thee; in the presence of the sons of my people give I it thee: bury thy dead. 12And Abraham bowed down himself before the people of the land. 13And he spake unto Ephron in the audience of the people of the land, saying, But if thou wilt give it, I pray thee, hear me: I will give thee money for the field; take it of me, and I will bury my dead there. 14And Ephron answered Abraham, saying unto him, 15My lord, hearken unto me: the land is worth four hundred shekels of silver; what is that betwixt me and thee? bury therefore thy dead. 16And Abraham hearkened unto Ephron; and Abraham weighed to Ephron the silver, which he had named in the audience of the sons of Heth, four hundred shekels of silver, current money with the merchant.

17And the field of Ephron, which was in Machpelah, which was before Mamre, the field, and the cave which was therein, and all the trees that were in the field, that were in all the borders round about, were made sure 18Unto Abraham for a possession in the presence of the children of Heth, before all that went in at the gate of his city. 19And after this, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah before Mamre: the same is Hebron in the land of Canaan. 20And the field, and the cave that is therein, were made sure unto Abraham for a possession of a buryingplace by the sons of Heth.

Commentary: A Grave Bought in Hope

Sarah, Sweetheart of the Bible A Spiritual Revelation, Not a Convenience Buried Near Job
Genesis 23 is a quiet chapter on its surface — a death, a negotiation, a burial. Brother Branham read it as something considerably larger: the first recorded instance of a pattern of deliberate burial choices running through the patriarchs, one he believed testified to their settled hope in a coming resurrection.

Sarah, Sweetheart of the Bible

Verses 1 and 2 record Sarah's death at a hundred and twenty-seven, and Abraham's open grief. Brother Branham spoke of Abraham and Sarah with unusual warmth among the patriarchal couples he preached on, calling them, more than once, the Bible's own picture of enduring devotion.
“
“Along came many other of the prophets. We'll take, for instance, Abraham, Sarah, the sweethearts of the Bible. And when Abraham come along, loving Sarah, when she died he just didn't bury her anywhere. He had a spiritual revelation of something.”
— William Branham, “Glorified Jesus” (55-0225, para. 36)

A Spiritual Revelation, Not a Convenience

Verses 3 through 16 record the careful, public negotiation for the cave of Machpelah — Abraham insisting on paying full price rather than accepting the field as a gift, so the ground would be unquestionably his own. No sourced commentary on the specific back-and-forth with Ephron, the sons of Heth, or the exact sum paid turned up in the corpus searched for this study. What Brother Branham did preach on repeatedly was Abraham's motive: this was not a practical decision about where land happened to be available, but a deliberate choice tied to something he had come to understand the same way the patriarch Job had understood it generations earlier.
“
“Job, when he died, he specified his burying place. Along come Abraham. Watch between the lines now, you readers. This is not written right in the Word; you have to read it in between…And when Sarah died, he bought a parcel of ground: bought it. You know how he did with a shekels of silver, near Job. And he bury her.”
— William Branham, “Demonology” (53-1112, para. 33)

Buried Near Job

Verses 17 through 20 close the chapter with the transaction confirmed and Sarah buried in the cave before Mamre. Brother Branham traced this same deliberate pattern forward through the rest of Genesis — Isaac buried with Abraham, Jacob buried in the same ground, and Joseph, centuries later in Egypt, making Israel swear to carry his own bones back to that same land rather than leave them behind.
“
“When Job died, he specified where to be buried. Wonder why? They buried Job. Along come Abraham years later. When Abraham died, or Sarah died, Abraham went right straight into the same land where Job was buried, and bought a piece of ground, and buried Sarah…Abraham begot Isaac. When Isaac died, he was buried with Abraham…When Joseph died, he said, 'Don't bury me here…Someday, God will visit you, then take my bones up in the promised land and bury them.'”
— William Branham, “God Perfecting His Church” (54-1204, paras. 60–61)
He read the choice of that particular ground as an act of resurrection faith rather than sentiment — a conviction, shared across generations of the same family, that Palestine specifically, not Egypt or anywhere else, was where the promised Redeemer would one day stand and where the dead in that hope would rise.
“
“Those prophets knew that the firstfruits of the resurrection would come out of Palestine, not out of Egypt, Palestine! The resurrection wasn't coming to Egypt; it was coming to Palestine. That's the reason they wanted to be buried in Palestine.”
— William Branham, “Glorified Jesus” (55-0225, para. 42)
Type & Shadow — A Grave Chosen in Hope
Brother Branham read the unbroken chain of burials in the cave of Machpelah — Sarah, then Abraham, then Isaac, Rebekah, Leah, and finally Jacob himself, brought back from Egypt for it — as a quiet but deliberate testimony running underneath the entire patriarchal narrative. None of them left instructions to be buried wherever convenient; each one insisted on that particular ground, in Job's country, in the land the promise was given over. In his reading, this was the "reading between the lines" evidence that Job's own confession, "I know that my Redeemer liveth, and he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth," was shared and acted upon by the patriarchs who came after him, generations before that resurrection hope was ever explained in doctrine.
