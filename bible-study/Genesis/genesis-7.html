Genesis

Chapter Seven

King James Version
And the LORD said unto Noah, Come thou and all thy house into the ark; for thee have I seen righteous before me in this generation. 2Of every clean beast thou shalt take to thee by sevens, the male and his female: and of beasts that are not clean by two, the male and his female. 3Of fowls also of the air by sevens, the male and the female; to keep seed alive upon the face of all the earth. 4For yet seven days, and I will cause it to rain upon the earth forty days and forty nights; and every living substance that I have made will I destroy from off the face of the earth.

5And Noah did according unto all that the LORD commanded him. 6And Noah was six hundred years old when the flood of waters was upon the earth. 7And Noah went in, and his sons, and his wife, and his sons' wives with him, into the ark, because of the waters of the flood. 8Of clean beasts, and of beasts that are not clean, and of fowls, and of every thing that creepeth upon the earth, 9There went in two and two unto Noah into the ark, the male and the female, as God had commanded Noah. 10And it came to pass after seven days, that the waters of the flood were upon the earth.

11In the six hundredth year of Noah's life, in the second month, the seventeenth day of the month, the same day were all the fountains of the great deep broken up, and the windows of heaven were opened. 12And the rain was upon the earth forty days and forty nights.

13In the selfsame day entered Noah, and Shem, and Ham, and Japheth, the sons of Noah, and Noah's wife, and the three wives of his sons with them, into the ark; 14They, and every beast after his kind, and all the cattle after their kind, and every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind, and every fowl after his kind, every bird of every sort. 15And they went in unto Noah into the ark, two and two of all flesh, wherein is the breath of life. 16And they that went in, went in male and female of all flesh, as God had commanded him: and the LORD shut him in.

17And the flood was forty days upon the earth; and the waters increased, and bare up the ark, and it was lift up above the earth. 18And the waters prevailed, and were increased greatly upon the earth; and the ark went upon the face of the waters. 19And the waters prevailed exceedingly upon the earth; and all the high hills, that were under the whole heaven, were covered. 20Fifteen cubits upward did the waters prevail; and the mountains were covered.

21And all flesh died that moved upon the earth, both of fowl, and of cattle, and of beast, and of every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth, and every man: 22All in whose nostrils was the breath of life, of all that was in the dry land, died. 23And every living substance was destroyed which was upon the face of the ground, both man, and cattle, and the creeping things, and the fowl of the heaven; and they were destroyed from the earth: and Noah only remained alive, and they that were with him in the ark. 24And the waters prevailed upon the earth an hundred and fifty days.

Commentary: The Pitch, the Shut Door, and the Waters That Judged

The Pitch That Atones Nothing Left to Do But Walk In The LORD Shut Him In Life Went On Outside Two Judgments: Water, Then Fire
Chapter 6 gave the ark's blueprint; this chapter is where it is finally used. Everything Brother Branham said about the ark being a type of Christ gets tested here, in the one moment that matters — the door closing, the rain starting, and the difference between who is inside and who is not.

The Pitch That Atones

Genesis 6:14 had commanded Noah to pitch the ark within and without, and Brother Branham went back to that instruction to explain why the ark could ride out the very judgment this chapter describes falling on everyone left outside it.
“
“The word ‘pitch’ means ‘to atone.’ Over in Leviticus, the same word that was used for pitch in Genesis, in Leviticus is used as an atone, to make an atonement…And the pitch that was in the gopher wood, in the ark, was to ‘keep out,’ or to atone the believer from the wrath.”
— William Branham, “The Ark” (55-0522, para. 90)
“
“Noah could not get into the wrath, the waters of judgment…He could not come into the judgment, because there was an atonement between him and the judgment. And the judgment was the water of the world.”
— William Branham, “The Ark” (55-0522, para. 91)
As verses 17 through 20 describe the waters rising until every high hill was covered, the ark itself never has to fight the flood on its own strength. What stands between Noah and the water is not the thickness of the wood, but what the wood was coated with.

Nothing Left to Do But Walk In

Verses 7 through 9 record the actual entering — Noah, his family, and the animals coming in two by two, exactly as commanded. Brother Branham pointed out how little Noah himself contributed to his own safety at this exact moment.
“
“The believer, in Christ, is safely secured…not upon the merits of himself, but on his obedience, obedience to walk into the ark. The only thing Noah had to do was get in the ark. He didn't even have to shut the door, God did that Himself. Walk in! That's all the believer does, is walk by faith in Christ. God takes care of the rest of it.”
— William Branham, “The Ark” (55-0522, para. 92)

The LORD Shut Him In

Verse 16 ends on a detail easy to read past: not that Noah shut the door, but that the LORD shut him in. Brother Branham treated the identity of who closes that door as the whole point of the verse.
“
“Notice, Noah, when he came in. That's the only thing he had to do, was walk in. God shut the door, and he was safely until the judgments was passed. God done the shutting; God done the opening. That's the believer, today, in Christ.”
— William Branham, “The Ark” (55-0522, para. 102)
Type & Shadow — A Door No Man Closes
Verse 16 gives Noah no part in the shutting of the door, only in the walking through it. Security in the ark rests on whose hand closed it, not on Noah's ability to keep it closed himself. Once God shuts a door, the flood outside cannot reopen it, and nothing inside needs to hold it shut either.

Life Went On Outside

Verse 10 notes seven days between the command and the rain, and even after the door was shut, the flood did not fall instantly. Brother Branham drew attention to how ordinary those final days must have looked to everyone still outside the ark.
“
“Like Noah, he went in, the ark shut behind him, nothing happened for a while; but the world perished on the outside, and the world went right on living just the same.”
— William Branham, “The World Is Again Falling Apart” (63-1127, para. 222)
The door being shut and the judgment actually falling were, on this reading, two separate moments — and the gap between them was no evidence to the world outside that anything had changed, right up until it had.

Two Judgments: Water, Then Fire

Verses 21 through 23 record total destruction — every living thing outside the ark, without exception. Brother Branham preached this flood as the first of two judgments Scripture points to, with a second, different judgment still to come on the present age.
“
“As it was in the days of Noah, so will it be, only this time instead of water it'll be fire. The world will be rocked by atomic power.”
— William Branham, “The Pool of Bethesda” (50-0811, para. 2)
“
“As it was in the days of Noah, when that little group was just saved by water, so shall it be in the coming of the Son of man. The days of Lot, when…In Noah's time, ‘They were eating, drinking, marrying, given in marriage.’ And look at it today.”
— William Branham, “Confirmation of the Commission” (62-0122, para. 295)
“
“Not in Noah's time, as the flood (He give the immorals of that day), but He said, ‘As it was in the days of Sodom.’ Now that was a Gentile world that was destroyed with fire. God put a rainbow in the sky, no more ‘water,’ but it's fire this time.”
— William Branham, “When Their Eyes Were Opened They Knew Him” (64-0312, para. 138)
On this reading, Genesis 7 is not a closed, one-time event so much as the first half of a pair: a world judged by water once, under God's own promise never to repeat that particular judgment, and a second world — this one — still standing under warning of a different judgment still ahead.

Scripture Cross-References
