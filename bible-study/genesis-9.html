Genesis

Chapter Nine

King James Version
And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth. 2And the fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon every beast of the earth, and upon every fowl of the air, upon all that moveth upon the earth, and upon all the fishes of the sea; into your hand are they delivered. 3Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you; even as the green herb have I given you all things. 4But flesh with the life thereof, which is the blood thereof, shall ye not eat. 5And surely your blood of your lives will I require; at the hand of every beast will I require it, and at the hand of man; at the hand of every man's brother will I require the life of man. 6Whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man. 7And you, be ye fruitful, and multiply; bring forth abundantly in the earth, and multiply therein.

8And God spake unto Noah, and to his sons with him, saying, 9And I, behold, I establish my covenant with you, and with your seed after you; 10And with every living creature that is with you, of the fowl, of the cattle, and of every beast of the earth with you; from all that go out of the ark, to every beast of the earth. 11And I will establish my covenant with you; neither shall all flesh be cut off any more by the waters of a flood; neither shall there any more be a flood to destroy the earth.

12And God said, This is the token of the covenant which I make between me and you and every living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations: 13I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth. 14And it shall come to pass, when I bring a cloud over the earth, that the bow shall be seen in the cloud: 15And I will remember my covenant, which is between me and you and every living creature of all flesh; and the waters shall no more become a flood to destroy all flesh. 16And the bow shall be in the cloud; and I will look upon it, that I may remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is upon the earth. 17And God said unto Noah, This is the token of the covenant, which I have established between me and all flesh that is upon the earth.

18And the sons of Noah, that went forth of the ark, were Shem, and Ham, and Japheth: and Ham is the father of Canaan. 19These are the three sons of Noah: and of them was the whole earth overspread.

20And Noah began to be an husbandman, and he planted a vineyard: 21And he drank of the wine, and was drunken; and he was uncovered within his tent. 22And Ham, the father of Canaan, saw the nakedness of his father, and told his two brethren without. 23And Shem and Japheth took a garment, and laid it upon both their shoulders, and went backward, and covered the nakedness of their father; and their faces were backward, and they saw not their father's nakedness.

24And Noah awoke from his wine, and knew what his younger son had done unto him. 25And he said, Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be unto his brethren. 26And he said, Blessed be the LORD God of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant. 27God shall enlarge Japheth, and he shall dwell in the tents of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant.

28And Noah lived after the flood three hundred and fifty years. 29And all the days of Noah were nine hundred and fifty years: and he died.

Study: The Sign, the Vine, and the Curse

The world Noah steps into needs new terms, and God restates the original mandate with real differences. "Be fruitful and multiply" returns from Genesis 1, but this time animals fear man rather than simply being named by him, meat is explicitly permitted for the first time, and a hard line is drawn at blood. Verse 6 goes further still, grounding the first instance of human justice — capital punishment for murder — not in law or convenience, but in the fact that man is made in the image of God. That's a strong foundation for something as serious as taking a life in judgment.
Then comes a promise that doesn't depend on anyone's behavior at all — unconditional, and, remarkably, aimed as much at the earth itself and at God's own resolve as at Noah personally.
“
“'I do set my bow in the clouds, and it shall be for a token of a covenant betwixt me and the earth.' What? 'Betwixt Me and Noah'? No. 'Betwixt Me and the earth.'... This was God's Own covenant with Himself, a rainbow... A token of what? Of a sacrifice that has been accepted” — pointing straight back to Noah's altar in the previous chapter.
— William Branham, “Revelation, Chapter Four, Part II” (61-0101)
That same sermon carries the rainbow further, into typology many readers won't have considered: the seven visible colors as a picture of seven church ages, and this rainbow's limited, half-arc appearance as a contrast to something greater still to come.
“
“Noah's rainbow composed primarily of seven colors... seven bows, seven churches reflecting seven lights, each light wrapped into the other one... a covenant that through Seven Church Ages... He would save the earth. But... this rainbow only, horizontally, just covers one half the earth... When John saw Him [in Revelation], He surrounded the whole Throne of God.”
— William Branham, “Revelation, Chapter Four, Part II” (61-0101)
The chapter then turns, abruptly and without transition, from a cosmic covenant to a very small, very human failure. Noah — the one man found righteous enough to survive the judgment of the entire world — plants a vineyard, drinks its wine, and ends up drunk and exposed inside his own tent. Nothing in the text softens that. The man who walked with God, who built the ark exactly as instructed, who offered the sacrifice that made a covenant with the whole earth possible, is shown here at his most undignified.
What his three sons each do with that moment says something plain about honor. Ham sees his father's condition and goes and tells his brothers — making his father's shame into information to pass around rather than something to cover. Shem and Japheth do the opposite: they take a garment between them, walk in backward so as not to see what they're covering, and protect their father's dignity without so much as looking at what caused the problem in the first place.
What follows — Noah's curse on Canaan and blessing on Shem and Japheth — is one of the most misused passages in the entire Bible, so it's worth being exact about what the text actually says and doesn't say. The curse falls on Canaan, one of Ham's sons, not on Ham himself, and certainly not on any race or people group; the words "race," "color," and "skin" appear nowhere in this passage. Ham had at least three other sons — Cush, Mizraim, and Phut, named in the very next chapter — and none of them are cursed here at all. For centuries this text was twisted to justify slavery and racial hierarchy, a reading the passage itself simply does not support once you read what it actually says rather than what tradition has claimed it says.
A note on sourcing, for the sake of being consistent with how this whole study has worked: unlike almost every other passage covered so far, I couldn't find anywhere in Brother Branham's sermons where he directly addresses this specific episode — the drunkenness, the curse on Canaan, or the historical misuse of this passage. Where his own teaching runs out, this study runs out with it rather than guessing at what he might have said.
The chapter, and Noah's whole story, closes on a simple accounting: three hundred fifty more years lived after the flood, nine hundred fifty years total, and then he died — a man who outlasted an entire world, and lived long enough to see the beginnings of the next one take root.
