Genesis

Chapter Thirteen

King James Version
And Abram went up out of Egypt, he, and his wife, and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the south. 2And Abram was very rich in cattle, in silver, and in gold. 3And he went on his journeys from the south even to Bethel, unto the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Hai; 4Unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first: and there Abram called on the name of the LORD.

5And Lot also, which went with Abram, had flocks, and herds, and tents. 6And the land was not able to bear them, that they might dwell together: for their substance was great, so that they could not dwell together. 7And there was a strife between the herdmen of Abram's cattle and the herdmen of Lot's cattle: and the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land. 8And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdmen and thy herdmen; for we be brethren. 9Is not the whole land before thee? separate thyself, I pray thee, from me: if thou wilt take the left hand, then I will go to the right; or if thou depart to the right hand, then I will go to the left.

10And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where, before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah, even as the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt, as thou comest unto Zoar. 11Then Lot chose him all the plain of Jordan; and Lot journeyed east: and they separated themselves the one from the other. 12Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom. 13But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly.

14And the LORD said unto Abram, after that Lot was separated from him, Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward: 15For all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever. 16And I will make thy seed as the dust of the earth: so that if a man can number the dust of the earth, then shall thy seed also be numbered. 17Arise, walk through the land in the length of it and in the breadth of it; for I will give it thee.

18Then Abram removed his tent, and came and dwelt in the plain of Mamre, which is in Hebron, and built there an altar unto the LORD.

Study: What Two Sets of Eyes Saw

Abram comes back out of Egypt and goes straight back to the exact place he'd left — "unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first." After a chapter that ended with his nerve failing him badly, there's something quietly hopeful in that detail: he doesn't stay lost in Egypt's compromise. He retraces his steps to where he'd last called on the name of the LORD.
The trouble that follows isn't sin exactly — it's success. Both men have grown too prosperous for the land to support them together, and their herdsmen start fighting over it. Abram's handling of the conflict is generous almost to a fault: as the elder, the one who actually received the promise, he still offers Lot first pick of the whole land. Whatever else this chapter says about Abram, it's a real contrast to how he handled pressure just one chapter earlier.
Lot's choice, when it comes, is described entirely in terms of what his eyes could see: a well-watered plain, lush "even as the garden of the LORD." Nothing in the text says he consulted God about it at all.

Once Lot is gone, the text hands Abram his own moment of lifted eyes — and the echo is almost certainly deliberate. Lot "lifted up his eyes" and picked the best-looking land available to him. Now God tells Abram, "Lift up now thine eyes," and gives him something no amount of looking could have found on its own: not the most fertile plain in view, but "all the land which thou seest," and offspring beyond counting, promised in a word rather than chosen by sight. Two men look out over the same general region. One trusts what looks good. The other is handed something no landscape could show him.
Abram's response is to move again and build yet another altar, his third in two chapters, at Mamre in Hebron. Lot's story simply trails off for now, settled toward a city the text has already quietly flagged: "the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly." Nothing bad has happened to him yet. But the ground he's chosen has already been named for what it is, and the reader knows it even if Lot, for the moment, doesn't seem to.
