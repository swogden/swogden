Genesis

Chapter Twenty-One

King James Version
And the LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did unto Sarah as he had spoken. 2For Sarah conceived, and bare Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him. 3And Abraham called the name of his son that was born unto him, whom Sarah bare to him, Isaac. 4And Abraham circumcised his son Isaac being eight days old, as God had commanded him. 5And Abraham was an hundred years old, when his son Isaac was born unto him. 6And Sarah said, God hath made me to laugh, so that all that hear will laugh with me. 7And she said, Who would have said unto Abraham, that Sarah should have given children suck? for I have born him a son in his old age. 8And the child grew, and was weaned: and Abraham made a great feast the same day that Isaac was weaned.

9And Sarah saw the son of Hagar the Egyptian, which she had born unto Abraham, mocking. 10Wherefore she said unto Abraham, Cast out this bondwoman and her son: for the son of this bondwoman shall not be heir with my son, even with Isaac. 11And the thing was very grievous in Abraham's sight because of his son. 12And God said unto Abraham, Let it not be grievous in thy sight because of the lad, and because of thy bondwoman; in all that Sarah hath said unto thee, hearken unto her voice; for in Isaac shall thy seed be called. 13And also of the son of the bondwoman will I make a nation, because he is thy seed.

14And Abraham rose up early in the morning, and took bread, and a bottle of water, and gave it unto Hagar, putting it on her shoulder, and the child, and sent her away: and she departed, and wandered in the wilderness of Beersheba. 15And the water was spent in the bottle, and she cast the child under one of the shrubs. 16And she went, and sat her down over against him a good way off, as it were a bowshot: for she said, Let me not see the death of the child. And she sat over against him, and lift up her voice, and wept.

17And God heard the voice of the lad; and the angel of God called to Hagar out of heaven, and said unto her, What aileth thee, Hagar? fear not; for God hath heard the voice of the lad where he is. 18Arise, lift up the lad, and hold him in thine hand; for I will make him a great nation. 19And God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water; and she went, and filled the bottle with water, and gave the lad drink. 20And God was with the lad; and he grew, and dwelt in the wilderness, and became an archer. 21And he dwelt in the wilderness of Paran: and his mother took him a wife out of the land of Egypt.

22And it came to pass at that time, that Abimelech and Phichol the chief captain of his host spake unto Abraham, saying, God is with thee in all that thou doest: 23Now therefore swear unto me here by God that thou wilt not deal falsely with me, nor with my son, nor with my son's son: but according to the kindness that I have done unto thee, thou shalt do unto me, and to the land wherein thou hast sojourned. 24And Abraham said, I will swear.

25And Abraham reproved Abimelech because of a well of water, which Abimelech's servants had violently taken away. 26And Abimelech said, I wot not who hath done this thing: neither didst thou tell me, neither yet heard I of it, but to day. 27And Abraham took sheep and oxen, and gave them unto Abimelech; and both of them made a covenant. 28And Abraham set seven ewe lambs of the flock by themselves. 29And Abimelech said unto Abraham, What mean these seven ewe lambs which thou hast set by themselves? 30And he said, For these seven ewe lambs shalt thou take of my hand, that they may be a witness unto me, that I have digged this well. 31Wherefore he called that place Beersheba; because there they sware both of them.

32Thus they made a covenant at Beersheba: then Abimelech rose up, and Phichol the chief captain of his host, and they returned into the land of the Philistines. 33And Abraham planted a grove in Beersheba, and called there on the name of the LORD, the everlasting God. 34And Abraham sojourned in the Philistines' land many days.

Commentary: The Promise Kept, and a Household Divided

The Promise Made Good Cast Out This Bondwoman Hagar's Well in the Wilderness The Covenant at Beersheba
After twenty-five years of waiting since the promise was first given, Genesis 21 records its plain fulfillment — and, within the same chapter, the painful separation the promise required.

The Promise Made Good

Verses 1 through 8 record the birth itself, the eighth-day circumcision required by chapter 17's covenant, and a family feast at Isaac's weaning. Brother Branham read the sequence as simple confirmation that God does exactly what He says, on His own timetable rather than a hurried one, and noted the milestones the text itself marks along the way.
“
“Here little Isaac was, when he was born, after Abraham was a hundred, and Sarah was nearly a hundred, about ninety years old. And God brought little Isaac into existence. And little Isaac was born. And when he was born, well, how happy the family was! They made a feast the day he was weaned. Abraham circumcised him after eight days.”
— William Branham, “God Hath a Provided Way” (56-0108, para. 143)

Cast Out This Bondwoman

Verses 9 through 14 record the conflict the weaning feast brought into the open: Ishmael mocking, Sarah's demand that Hagar and her son be sent away entirely, and Abraham's genuine grief at being asked to do it. God's own instruction settles the matter decisively — Sarah's request is upheld, because the covenant line runs through Isaac alone. As established in the study of Genesis 16 and 17, Brother Branham read Hagar and Sarah as successive types of the bondwoman and the freewoman in Galatians 4, and this chapter is where that separation is finally carried out in the narrative itself. No sourced commentary on Abraham's own grief in verse 11, specifically, turned up in the corpus searched for this study beyond what was already covered in the Genesis 16 study of the wider Hagar-and-Sarah typology.

Hagar's Well in the Wilderness

Verses 15 through 21 follow Hagar and Ishmael into the wilderness of Beersheba, where their water runs out and Hagar, unable to watch her son die, sits at a distance and weeps. God hears the boy's voice, opens Hagar's eyes to a well already there, and preserves both their lives. Brother Branham identified the source of that provision the same way he identified the Rock Moses struck in the wilderness — as Christ Himself, present and providing before His own incarnation.
“
“He is that Rock, that was…Hagar's Rock, in the time of trouble; when her baby was about to die, when she had been put out of the camp, and was out there with little Ishmael. Her water, in her cistern that she was packing with her, had give out. And she laid little Ishmael down; and walked over, a bow shot, and cried…And all at once, the Angel of the Lord spoke, and she found Beersheba, the well there that was flowing, and still flowing to this day. He was Hagar's Beersheba, Rock out there in the wilderness.”
— William Branham, “Broken Cisterns” (64-0726E, para. 111)
Type & Shadow — The Rock in the Wilderness
Brother Branham read the same identification of Christ as "the Rock" across multiple wilderness-water scenes in Scripture — Hagar's well at Beersheba here, and the rock Moses struck in Exodus and Numbers, both providing water where none existed and both, in his reading, pointing to the pre-incarnate Christ meeting desperate need before the incarnation itself. The pattern he drew was consistent: a cast-off or exhausted people, cut off from every human resource, provided for directly by the same Person who would later be struck for the sins of the world.

The Covenant at Beersheba

Verses 22 through 34 close the chapter with Abimelech and his captain Phichol seeking a formal covenant with Abraham, a dispute over a stolen well resolved, and Abraham planting a grove and calling on the LORD as "the everlasting God" at Beersheba. No sourced Branham commentary on this specific covenant scene — the treaty terms, the seven ewe lambs, or the naming of Beersheba in this context — turned up in the corpus searched for this study; the text is left to stand as Scripture presents it.

Scripture Cross-References

Galatians 4:22–31 — the bondwoman and the freewoman, and the command to "cast out" applied allegorically  •  Romans 9:7–9 — "in Isaac shall thy seed be called"  •  Hebrews 11:11–12 — Sarah's faith to conceive, "as good as dead"  •  Exodus 17:5–6 — the Rock struck at Horeb, water for a perishing people  •  1 Corinthians 10:4 — "that Rock was Christ"  •  Genesis 26:26–33 — Isaac's own later covenant with Abimelech at the same wells
