Genesis

Chapter Four

King James Version
And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD. 2And she again bare his brother Abel. And Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a tiller of the ground.

3And in process of time it came to pass, that Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD. 4And Abel, he also brought of the firstlings of his flock and of the fat thereof. And the LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering: 5But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect. And Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell.

6And the LORD said unto Cain, Why art thou wroth? and why is thy countenance fallen? 7If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him.

8And Cain talked with Abel his brother: and it came to pass, when they were in the field, that Cain rose up against Abel his brother, and slew him.

9And the LORD said unto Cain, Where is Abel thy brother? And he said, I know not: Am I my brother's keeper? 10And he said, What hast thou done? the voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground. 11And now art thou cursed from the earth, which hath opened her mouth to receive thy brother's blood from thy hand; 12When thou tillest the ground, it shall not henceforth yield unto thee her strength; a fugitive and a vagabond shalt thou be in the earth.

13And Cain said unto the LORD, My punishment is greater than I can bear. 14Behold, thou hast driven me out this day from the face of the earth; and from thy face shall I be hid; and I shall be a fugitive and a vagabond in the earth; and it shall come to pass, that every one that findeth me shall slay me. 15And the LORD said unto him, Therefore whosoever slayeth Cain, vengeance shall be taken on him sevenfold. And the LORD set a mark upon Cain, lest any finding him should kill him. 16And Cain went out from the presence of the LORD, and dwelt in the land of Nod, on the east of Eden.

17And Cain knew his wife; and she conceived, and bare Enoch: and he builded a city, and called the name of the city, after the name of his son, Enoch. 18And unto Enoch was born Irad: and Irad begat Mehujael: and Mehujael begat Methusael: and Methusael begat Lamech. 19And Lamech took unto him two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the other Zillah. 20And Adah bare Jabal: he was the father of such as dwell in tents, and of such as have cattle. 21And his brother's name was Jubal: he was the father of all such as handle the harp and organ. 22And Zillah, she also bare Tubalcain, an instructer of every artificer in brass and iron: and the sister of Tubalcain was Naamah.

23And Lamech said unto his wives, Adah and Zillah, Hear my voice; ye wives of Lamech, hearken unto my speech: for I have slain a man to my wounding, and a young man to my hurt. 24If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold.

25And Adam knew his wife again; and she bare a son, and called his name Seth: For God, said she, hath appointed me another seed instead of Abel, whom Cain slew. 26And then began men to call upon the name of the LORD.

Study: Two Offerings, Two Seeds

Read plainly, verse 1 says only that Adam knew Eve and she conceived and bore Cain. But this is the chapter where the promise of Genesis 3:15 — enmity between two seeds — starts to take on flesh, and it is worth stating plainly rather than skirting around it: this is the chapter Brother Branham's Serpent's Seed teaching is built on. He read Cain's very origin, not just his actions, as the root of everything that follows.
“
“...she was already pregnant, by Satan. And she brought forth her first son, whose name was Cain, the son of Satan.”
— William Branham, “The Serpent's Seed” (58-0928E)
Whatever a reader makes of that claim, it is the lens this teaching holds up to the rest of the chapter: two brothers, two natures, two altars. Cain brings the fruit of the ground — the same category of thing that had already caused trouble once in this book. Abel brings a lamb, blood, a life laid down. One offering is accepted; the other is not.
“
“He builds him a church, makes him an offering... brought his apples in, out of the field, laid them on there, said, ‘This will make an atonement.’ God said, ‘It wasn't apples.’ But, by spiritual revelation, Abel knew it was blood. So he brought a lamb... and God said, ‘That's right. That's what done it. It was blood.’”
— William Branham, “The Serpent's Seed” (58-0928E)
Notice, too, that God does not abandon Cain the moment his offering is refused. Verse 7 is a genuine warning, and a genuine offer — the door is not yet shut.
“
“Now, if you'll do well, it'll be all right; you'll be accepted. But if you don't do well, then sin lieth at the door.”
— William Branham, “Father, The Hour Has Come” (56-1002A)
Cain does not do well. Where Adam and Eve, guilty, at least hid themselves in Genesis 3, Cain lies outright when God asks after his brother — "Am I my brother's keeper?" — and the ground itself is left to testify against him, Abel's blood "crying" from the soil that drank it.
The sentence that follows is not death, but something closer to Adam and Eve's own exile: cursed from the ground, a fugitive and a vagabond, and yet marked and protected rather than handed over to be killed himself.
“
“Cain received the mark of the beast and was sent away... God sent, marked Cain, and as soon as Cain was marked, he went out of the Presence of the Lord.”
— William Branham, “The Mark of the Beast and the Seal of God 2” (61-0217)
The rest of the chapter reads almost like a ledger of what Cain's line built once it settled east of Eden: a city, herdsmen, musicians, workers in bronze and iron — civilization advancing rapidly, right alongside Lamech's boast of a vengeance far exceeding what was ever promised to Cain. And then, quietly, in the chapter's last two verses, the other line reasserts itself: Seth is born "instead of Abel," and it is only then that men begin, for the first time, to call upon the name of the LORD. Two seeds, two directions, both visible from the very first family.
