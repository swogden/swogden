Genesis

Chapter Thirty-Three

King James Version
And Jacob lifted up his eyes, and looked, and, behold, Esau came, and with him four hundred men. And he divided the children unto Leah, and unto Rachel, and unto the two handmaids. 2And he put the handmaids and their children foremost, and Leah and her children after, and Rachel and Joseph hindermost. 3And he passed over before them, and bowed himself to the ground seven times, until he came near to his brother.

4And Esau ran to meet him, and embraced him, and fell on his neck, and kissed him: and they wept. 5And he lifted up his eyes, and saw the women and the children; and said, Who are those with thee? And he said, The children which God hath graciously given thy servant. 6Then the handmaidens came near, they and their children, and they bowed themselves. 7And Leah also with her children came near, and bowed themselves: and after came Joseph near and Rachel, and they bowed themselves.

8And he said, What meanest thou by all this drove which I met? And he said, These are to find grace in the sight of my lord. 9And Esau said, I have enough, my brother; keep that thou hast unto thyself. 10And Jacob said, Nay, I pray thee, if now I have found grace in thy sight, then receive my present at my hand: for therefore I have seen thy face, as though I had seen the face of God, and thou wast pleased with me. 11Take, I pray thee, my blessing that is brought to thee; because God hath dealt graciously with me, and because I have enough. And he urged him, and he took it.

12And he said, Let us take our journey, and let us go, and I will go before thee. 13And he said unto him, My lord knoweth that the children are tender, and the flocks and herds with young are with me: and if men should overdrive them one day, all the flock will die. 14Let my lord, I pray thee, pass over before his servant: and I will lead on softly, according as the cattle that goeth before me and the children be able to endure, until I come unto my lord unto Seir.

15And Esau said, Let me now leave with thee some of the folk that are with me. And he said, What needeth it? let me find grace in the sight of my lord. 16So Esau returned that day on his way unto Seir. 17And Jacob journeyed to Succoth, and built him an house, and made booths for his cattle: therefore the name of the place is called Succoth.

18And Jacob came to Shalem, a city of Shechem, which is in the land of Canaan, when he came from Padanaram; and pitched his tent before the city. 19And he bought a parcel of a field, where he had spread his tent, at the hand of the children of Hamor, Shechem's father, for an hundred pieces of money. 20And he erected there an altar, and called it EleloheIsrael.

Commentary: The Fruit of a Night's Wrestling

He Didn't Need Any Guards Desperation Until Assurance Seeing Thy Face as the Face of God
Genesis 33 answers the fear that opened Genesis 32. The four hundred men Jacob dreaded meeting turn out to be an escort for a brother who runs to embrace him, and the elaborate seven-fold bow and staged procession of wives and children prove unnecessary against Esau's simple, tearful welcome. Brother Branham's most direct comment on this chapter reaches back to the previous night's wrestling match to explain exactly why Jacob could walk into this meeting differently than he had feared.

He Didn't Need Any Guards

Having preached at length on Jacob's desperate, all-night wrestling at Peniel, Brother Branham drew a direct line from that encounter to this one: the assurance Jacob received in the dark determined how he walked out to meet Esau in the daylight. The man who divided his household into two companies out of fear in chapter 32 needed no such precaution once the blessing had actually come.
“
“Jacob stayed there until the blessing came, and changed his name from 'a deceiver' to 'a prince with God.' Even the nation was called by his name. Yes, sir! What was it? The results was because he got desperate about the thing. And the next day when he met Esau, he didn't need any guards. He walked right out and met him. See? Why? He had got in desperation till he got the assurance. And you get desperate until you get the assurance.”
— William Branham, “Desperations” (63-0901E, para. 90)
Read this way, the careful staging Jacob still goes through in verses 1 through 3 — the handmaids first, Leah next, Rachel and Joseph last, and Jacob himself bowing seven times as he approaches — is less an act of fear than a formality left over from the night before, a habit of caution not yet fully shed even after the assurance had come. It is Esau, not Jacob, who breaks the formality first, running to meet him before any of the careful arrangement has a chance to matter.
Type & Shadow — A Face Like the Face of God
Jacob's own words in verse 10 tie this chapter back to the one before it in Scripture's own language, not only in Brother Branham's reading of it: "I have seen thy face, as though I had seen the face of God." The same man who, hours earlier, named the place of his wrestling Peniel — "I have seen God face to face" — now uses nearly identical language for the brother he had wronged and feared. Reconciliation with the brother he had cheated becomes, in Jacob's own words, an echo of the reconciliation he had just been given with God.

Esau's Grace and Jacob's Own Caution

Esau's response throughout this chapter — running to embrace his brother, weeping, refusing the elaborate gift at first, and offering to travel alongside Jacob or leave guards for his protection — stands in sharp contrast to the calculating, fearful brother pictured twenty years earlier in Genesis 27. No sourced Branham commentary specifically addressing Esau's own change of heart, or his generous conduct throughout this chapter, turned up in the corpus searched for this study.
Jacob's insistence on a separate, slower pace in verses 12 through 17, and his declining of Esau's offered escort, is left by Scripture without stated motive, and no sourced Branham commentary addressing Jacob's reasons for parting ways with Esau rather than traveling on together turned up in the corpus searched for this study. The chapter's closing purchase of land at Shechem and the altar called El-elohe-Israel — "God, the God of Israel," the first time this new name is used of God Himself rather than only of Jacob — likewise did not turn up separate direct commentary in the corpus searched for this study.

Scripture Cross-References

Genesis 32:6–8 — Jacob's fear and the division into two companies, now shown unnecessary  •  Genesis 32:30 — "I have seen God face to face," echoed in Jacob's words to Esau in verse 10  •  Genesis 27:41 — Esau's earlier vow to kill Jacob, now resolved in embrace rather than violence  •  Luke 15:20 — the father who "ran" to meet his returning son, the same verb used of Esau here  •  Romans 12:19–21 — overcoming evil with good, the pattern Esau's own conduct displays  •  Genesis 34:1–2 — the events at Shechem that follow directly from Jacob's settling near that city
