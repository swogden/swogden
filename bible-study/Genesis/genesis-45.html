Genesis

Chapter Forty-Five

King James Version
Then Joseph could not refrain himself before all them that stood by him; and he cried, Cause every man to go out from me. And there stood no man with him, while Joseph made himself known unto his brethren. 2And he wept aloud: and the Egyptians and the house of Pharaoh heard.

3And Joseph said unto his brethren, I am Joseph; doth my father yet live? And his brethren could not answer him; for they were troubled at his presence. 4And Joseph said unto his brethren, Come near to me, I pray you. And they came near. And he said, I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt. 5Now therefore be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life.

6For these two years hath the famine been in the land: and yet there are five years, in the which there shall neither be earing nor harvest. 7And God sent me before you to preserve you a posterity in the earth, and to save your lives by a great deliverance. 8So now it was not you that sent me hither, but God: and he hath made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and a ruler throughout all the land of Egypt.

9Haste ye, and go up to my father, and say unto him, Thus saith thy son Joseph, God hath made me lord of all Egypt: come down unto me, tarry not: 10And thou shalt dwell in the land of Goshen, and thou shalt be near unto me, thou, and thy children, and thy children's children, and thy flocks, and thy herds, and all that thou hast: 11And there will I nourish thee; for yet there are five years of famine; lest thou, and thy household, and all that thou hast, come to poverty.

12And, behold, your eyes see, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth that speaketh unto you. 13And ye shall tell my father of all my glory in Egypt, and of all that ye have seen; and ye shall haste and bring down my father hither. 14And he fell upon his brother Benjamin's neck, and wept; and Benjamin wept upon his neck. 15Moreover he kissed all his brethren, and wept upon them: and after that his brethren talked with him.

16And the fame thereof was heard in Pharaoh's house, saying, Joseph's brethren are come: and it pleased Pharaoh well, and his servants. 17And Pharaoh said unto Joseph, Say unto thy brethren, This do ye; lade your beasts, and go, get you unto the land of Canaan; 18And take your father and your households, and come unto me: and I will give you the good of the land of Egypt, and ye shall eat the fat of the land.

19Now thou art commanded, this do ye; take you wagons out of the land of Egypt for your little ones, and for your wives, and bring your father, and come. 20Also regard not your stuff; for the good of all the land of Egypt is your's.

21And the children of Israel did so: and Joseph gave them wagons, according to the commandment of Pharaoh, and gave them provision for the way. 22To all of them he gave each man changes of raiment; but to Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of silver, and five changes of raiment. 23And to his father he sent after this manner; ten asses laden with the good things of Egypt, and ten she asses laden with corn and bread and meat for his father by the way.

24So he sent his brethren away, and they departed: and he said unto them, See that ye fall not out by the way. 25And they went up out of Egypt, and came into the land of Canaan unto Jacob their father, 26And told him, saying, Joseph is yet alive, and he is governor over all the land of Egypt. And Jacob's heart fainted, for he believed them not.

27And they told him all the words of Joseph, which he had said unto them: and when he saw the wagons which Joseph had sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob their father revived: 28And Israel said, It is enough; Joseph my son is yet alive: I will go and see him before I die.

Commentary: I Am Joseph, Your Brother

Every Man Go From Me God Sent Me Before You Type & Shadow The Spirit of Jacob Revived
Genesis 45 is the moment the whole Joseph narrative has been building toward since Genesis 37, and three separate sermons, spanning 1953 to 1961, all converge on it. Brother Branham read this chapter as the clearest picture in all of Genesis of what he believed still lay ahead: Christ making Himself known to the Jewish people who had rejected Him.

Every Man Go From Me

Preaching in 1960, Brother Branham read most of verses 1 through 7 directly, pausing only to underline how completely Joseph's self-control finally broke — he had to clear the room of every Egyptian witness before he could speak, and even then his weeping was loud enough to be heard back in Pharaoh's own house.
“
Now remember, Joseph, making hisself known, he cried, “Every man go from before me.” And there stood no man with him, while Joseph made himself known to his brethren. And he wept aloud: and the Egyptians and the house of Pharaoh heard him. (He must have screamed out.) And Joseph said unto his brethren, I am Joseph; does my father yet live? And his brethren could not answer him; for they were troubled at his presence.
— William Branham, “The Revelation of Jesus Christ” (60-1204M, paras. 307–308)
Brother Branham read the same scene again the following year, giving special attention to Joseph's wife — absent from the moment of revealing, as he understood it — and to the brothers' terror once they realized who stood before them.
“
Watch now the 45th, verse…Listen close, don't miss it. Then Joseph could not refrain himself before all them that stood with him;…and he cried—and he…, Cause every man to go from me. And there stood no man with him, while Joseph made himself known to his brethren. What is it? Even his own wife went back to the palace…And he wept aloud:…Joseph just couldn't hold it any longer, and he screamed out…and the Egyptians and the house of Pharaoh heard it.
— William Branham, “The Sixfold Purpose of Gabriel's Visit to Daniel” (61-0730E, paras. 76–77)
Type & Shadow — Hated Without a Cause
Brother Branham read Joseph's whole life — hated by his own brothers for the very gift that set him apart, yet loved by his father — as a direct picture of Christ, hated by the religious leaders of His day for the same kind of gift: the ability to see, to foretell, to heal.
“
Now, Joseph, when he was born, he was hated by his brethren. Is that right? Now I want to show you, Joseph represents the Spirit-filled Church. Joseph was hated of his brethren. Why? Because he was spiritual. Joseph could not help because he could see vision. He could not help because he dreamed dreams, see, and could interpret dreams. He, that was what was in him. He could not display anything else but what that was in him. Well, then, his brothers hated him, without a cause. But his father loved him, because his father was a prophet.
— William Branham, “The Revelation of Jesus Christ” (60-1204M, para. 310)

God Sent Me Before You

Verse 5 — "be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life" — is Joseph's own theological reading of everything that happened to him, offered to the very men responsible for it. He does not deny what they did or minimize it; he reframes it underneath a larger purpose he can now see because he is standing on the other side of it. Preaching in 1953, Brother Branham returned to this same scene, narrating Joseph's revealing of himself and his embrace of Benjamin in his own words rather than by direct quotation, but landing on the same point: a man overcome with relief that his father was still alive, more than with any grievance against his brothers.
“
And he—he made them all leave, then he revealed himself, he said, “I am Joseph, your brother.” And they were scared, the patriarch. They was afraid. He said, “Don't be afraid.” And he begin to scream so loud till even over in Pharaoh's palace they heard him screaming, heard his screams and cry. He run down and throwed his arms around little Benjamin, and hugged him and kissed him, and fell on his neck and begin crying, said, “You mean to say that my poor old daddy's still alive up in Canaan?” What a feeling! What, the love of God that's shed abroad! “My daddy's still alive and I'm giving him some corn.” Said, “Oh!” And he screamed to the top of his voice.
— William Branham, “Israel and the Church 1” (53-0325, para. 190)

The Spirit of Jacob Revived

No sourced Branham commentary was found addressing the chapter's closing verses specifically — Jacob's initial disbelief, and his spirit reviving only once he sees the wagons Joseph sent. The report alone, "Joseph is yet alive," was not enough; it was the sight of tangible proof, wagon after wagon loaded with Egypt's abundance, that finally convinced a father who had mourned his son as dead for over twenty years.

Scripture Cross-References

Zechariah 12:10 — "they shall look upon me whom they have pierced," Brother Branham's own point of comparison for this scene  •  Acts 7:13 — Stephen's summary, "at the second time Joseph was made known to his brethren"  •  Romans 11:25–27 — a future turning of Israel, the doctrine this chapter is read to support  •  Genesis 37:4 — "they hated him, and could not speak peaceably unto him," the enmity now dissolved  •  Genesis 50:20 — Joseph's own later restatement of verse 5, "ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good"  •  John 20:20 — the disciples' own mixture of joy and fear at recognizing the risen Christ
