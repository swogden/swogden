Genesis

Chapter Eight

King James Version
And God remembered Noah, and every living thing, and all the cattle that was with him in the ark: and God made a wind to pass over the earth, and the waters asswaged; 2The fountains also of the deep and the windows of heaven were stopped, and the rain from heaven was restrained; 3And the waters returned from off the earth continually: and after the end of the hundred and fifty days the waters were abated.

4And the ark rested in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, upon the mountains of Ararat. 5And the waters decreased continually until the tenth month: in the tenth month, on the first day of the month, were the tops of the mountains seen.

6And it came to pass at the end of forty days, that Noah opened the window of the ark which he had made: 7And he sent forth a raven, which went forth to and fro, until the waters were dried up from off the earth. 8Also he sent forth a dove from him, to see if the waters were abated from off the face of the ground; 9But the dove found no rest for the sole of her foot, and she returned unto him into the ark, for the waters were on the face of the whole earth: then he put forth his hand, and took her, and pulled her in unto him into the ark.

10And he stayed yet other seven days; and again he sent forth the dove out of the ark; 11And the dove came in to him in the evening; and, lo, in her mouth was an olive leaf pluckt off: so Noah knew that the waters were abated from off the earth. 12And he stayed yet other seven days; and sent forth the dove; which returned not again unto him any more.

13And it came to pass in the six hundredth and first year, in the first month, the first day of the month, the waters were dried up from off the earth: and Noah removed the covering of the ark, and looked, and, behold, the face of the ground was dry. 14And in the second month, on the seven and twentieth day of the month, was the earth dried.

15And God spake unto Noah, saying, 16Go forth of the ark, thou, and thy wife, and thy sons, and thy sons' wives with thee. 17Bring forth with thee every living thing that is with thee, of all flesh, both of fowl, and of cattle, and of every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth; that they may breed abundantly in the earth, and be fruitful, and multiply upon the earth.

18And Noah went forth, and his sons, and his wife, and his sons' wives with him: 19Every beast, every creeping thing, and every fowl, and whatsoever creepeth upon the earth, after their kinds, went forth out of the ark.

20And Noah builded an altar unto the LORD; and took of every clean beast, and of every clean fowl, and offered burnt offerings on the altar. 21And the LORD smelled a sweet savour; and the LORD said in his heart, I will not again curse the ground any more for man's sake; for the imagination of man's heart is evil from his youth; neither will I again smite any more every thing living, as I have done.

22While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.

Commentary: The Crow, the Dove, and an Altar on Dry Ground

God Remembered Noah One Window, Looking Up The Crow and the Dove The Dove Used Twice Noah's Altar While the Earth Remaineth
The waters that judged in chapter 7 begin, in this chapter, to recede — and Brother Branham found in the ordinary details of Noah's waiting, watching, and finally stepping onto dry ground, a picture of the believer's own life inside Christ from the day of salvation to the day of testimony.

God Remembered Noah

Verse 1 opens the chapter on a single sentence — “God remembered Noah” — after which the water itself begins to go down. Brother Branham held Noah up as one of Scripture's clearest examples of a man with reason to remember what the Lord had done for him in turn.
“
“I'd imagine, way over in Glory tonight, there's a man by the name of Noah, and he certainly has a lot to remember the Lord for. For in the time that when God was going to destroy all the wickedness off of the face the earth, God remembered Noah. And Noah remembers how he escaped the wrath of God, by God's mercy.”
— William Branham, “Remembering the Lord” (63-0122, para. 46)

One Window, Looking Up

Verse 6 mentions “the window of the ark,” and Brother Branham noted a detail about the ark's construction that shaped everything Noah could and could not see during those long months afloat.
“
“The ark was so constructed, when you were once in it, there was only one window in it, and it was right in the top. You couldn't look sideways, you couldn't look no way but straight up. And that's the way the Ark, Jesus Christ, is. You can't look at the next fellow…you can't look at nothing but Christ when you're in the Ark because there's only one door.”
— William Branham, “On the Wings of a Snow-White Dove” (65-1128E, para. 93)
Everyone in the ark, however different their circumstances had been outside it, was looking at exactly the same thing through exactly the same opening: up, and nowhere else.

The Crow and the Dove

Verses 7 through 12 send out two different birds, with two very different outcomes, and Brother Branham built an entire doctrine of the born-again believer around the difference between them.
“
“He sent him out. He was a crow, and that crow never did return, because he found pleasure out there, outside the ark of God. He flew from one old dead body to another, eating…this dove was of a different nature…It was not a vulture, neither is it a scavenger, it can only eat the clean pure things. And it was so satisfied because it couldn't find nothing else to put the soles of his feet on, it returned back to the ark.”
— William Branham, “On the Wings of a Snow-White Dove” (65-1128E, paras. 94–95)
“
“The dove and the crow, both, was in the ark…When they turned the old crow loose; let's type that as backsliding. The old crow flew from dead body to dead body, and he picked on the old dead carcasses. He was all right. Didn't make any difference to him, 'cause he was a crow, to begin with. His nature, his appetite…could digest such a thing.”
— William Branham, “Divine Healing” (54-1219M, paras. 259–260)
“
“A dove hasn't got any gall. It can't digest those thing. And if it would eat them, it would kill them. But the dove, she would try to fly down; 'whew,' she couldn't stand it…Every man or woman that's born of the Spirit of God, sure, you can backslide. But, brother, if you're a child of God, you can't stand the things of the world no more, 'cause your digesting things has been changed.”
— William Branham, “Divine Healing” (54-1219M, paras. 262–263)
Type & Shadow — Two Birds, Two Natures
Both birds left the same ark through the same window into the same ruined world. Neither one was punished or rewarded for what it found out there; each one simply did what its own nature could do. The crow's nature could feed on a dead world and stay. The dove's nature could not, and came home. On this reading, the difference between the world's welcome and the believer's homesickness for Christ is not a matter of willpower, but of what has actually been changed on the inside.

The Dove Used Twice

Brother Branham did not treat the dove's appearance in this chapter as an isolated incident. He set it beside the one other moment in Scripture where a dove marks a turning point in redemption's story.
“
“God used a dove for…also used in Matthew 3:16, again when God's wrath was on the earth…there was no way to get out of it…But God used a dove again. It pleased Him, His Son Jesus had pleased Him so well, that He identified Him.”
— William Branham, “On the Wings of a Snow-White Dove” (65-1128E, paras. 97–98)
In the ark, the dove's return marked that the wrath outside had not yet lifted. At the Jordan, the dove's descent marked the opposite — the one Man in whom God found nothing that wrath needed to fall on. The same bird, in both cases, is Brother Branham's picture of the Spirit's own testimony to what it finds, or does not find, worth resting on.

Noah's Altar

Verses 20 and 21 record the first act Noah performs on dry ground: not settling his family, not surveying the ruined earth, but building an altar and offering a sacrifice before doing anything else. The chapter's very next line is God's own response — a promise never again to curse the ground or destroy every living thing as He had just done.

While the Earth Remaineth

Verse 22 closes the chapter with a promise of ongoing order — seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night — that Brother Branham read together with the fuller covenant language that immediately follows it in the next chapter.
“
“While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest,…cold and heat,…summer and winter,…day and night shall not cease. (A covenant.)”
— William Branham, “Revelation, Chapter Four, Part II” (61-0101, para. 174)
Brother Branham called this a covenant in its own right, spoken over the newly emptied earth before the more familiar sign of that covenant — the rainbow — is given in the verses that open the next chapter.

Scripture Cross-References

Matthew 3:16–17 — the Spirit of God descending like a dove, lighting upon Him  •  Genesis 9:11–13 — the covenant sign that follows this chapter's promise  •  2 Peter 2:22 — the dog turned to his own vomit again  •  2 Corinthians 5:17 — old things are passed away, all things are become new  •  Song of Solomon 2:14 — O my dove, that art in the clefts of the rock  •  Genesis 4:4 — Abel's own firstling offering, the pattern this altar repeats  •  Hebrews 13:15 — the sacrifice of praise to God continually
