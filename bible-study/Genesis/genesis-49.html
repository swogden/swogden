Genesis

Chapter Forty-Nine

King James Version
And Jacob called unto his sons, and said, Gather yourselves together, that I may tell you that which shall befall you in the last days. 2Gather yourselves together, and hear, ye sons of Jacob; and hearken unto Israel your father.

3Reuben, thou art my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my strength, the excellency of dignity, and the excellency of power: 4Unstable as water, thou shalt not excel; because thou wentest up to thy father's bed; then defiledst thou it: he went up to my couch.

5Simeon and Levi are brethren; instruments of cruelty are in their habitations. 6O my soul, come not thou into their secret; unto their assembly, mine honour, be not thou united: for in their anger they slew a man, and in their selfwill they digged down a wall. 7Cursed be their anger, for it was fierce; and their wrath, for it was cruel: I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel.

8Judah, thou art he whom thy brethren shall praise: thy hand shall be in the neck of thine enemies; thy father's children shall bow down before thee. 9Judah is a lion's whelp: from the prey, my son, thou art gone up: he stooped down, he couched as a lion, and as an old lion; who shall rouse him up? 10The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come; and unto him shall the gathering of the people be. 11Binding his foal unto the vine, and his ass's colt unto the choice vine; he washed his garments in wine, and his clothes in the blood of grapes: 12His eyes shall be red with wine, and his teeth white with milk.

13Zebulun shall dwell at the haven of the sea; and he shall be for an haven of ships; and his border shall be unto Zidon.

14Issachar is a strong ass couching down between two burdens: 15And he saw that rest was good, and the land that it was pleasant; and bowed his shoulder to bear, and became a servant unto tribute.

16Dan shall judge his people, as one of the tribes of Israel. 17Dan shall be a serpent by the way, an adder in the path, that biteth the horse heels, so that his rider shall fall backward. 18I have waited for thy salvation, O LORD.

19Gad, a troop shall overcome him: but he shall overcome at the last.

20Out of Asher his bread shall be fat, and he shall yield royal dainties.

21Naphtali is a hind let loose: he giveth goodly words.

22Joseph is a fruitful bough, even a fruitful bough by a well; whose branches run over the wall: 23The archers have sorely grieved him, and shot at him, and hated him: 24But his bow abode in strength, and the arms of his hands were made strong by the hands of the mighty God of Jacob; (from thence is the shepherd, the stone of Israel:) 25Even by the God of thy father, who shall help thee; and by the Almighty, who shall bless thee with blessings of heaven above, blessings of the deep that lieth under, blessings of the breasts, and of the womb: 26The blessings of thy father have prevailed above the blessings of my progenitors unto the utmost bound of the everlasting hills: they shall be on the head of Joseph, and on the crown of the head of him that was separate from his brethren.

27Benjamin shall ravin as a wolf: in the morning he shall devour the prey, and at night he shall divide the spoil.

28All these are the twelve tribes of Israel: and this is it that their father spake unto them, and blessed them; every one according to his blessing he blessed them.

29And he charged them, and said unto them, I am to be gathered unto my people: bury me with my fathers in the cave that is in the field of Ephron the Hittite, 30In the cave that is in the field of Machpelah, which is before Mamre, in the land of Canaan, which Abraham bought with the field of Ephron the Hittite for a possession of a buryingplace. 31There they buried Abraham and Sarah his wife; there they buried Isaac and Rebekah his wife; and there I buried Leah. 32The purchase of the field and of the cave that is therein was from the children of Heth.

33And when Jacob had made an end of commanding his sons, he gathered up his feet into the bed, and yielded up the ghost, and was gathered unto his people.

Commentary: Until Shiloh Come

Gather Yourselves Together

No sourced Branham commentary on verses 1–2 turned up in the corpus searched for this study. Jacob's opening words announce that what follows is not a father's private sentiment but prophecy: "that which shall befall you in the last days." What is delivered over the next twenty-six verses is addressed to each son individually, yet the whole is framed as one utterance concerning Israel's future as twelve distinct tribes, a framing the text confirms explicitly in verse 28.

Blessings Withheld

No sourced Branham commentary on verses 3–7 turned up in the corpus searched for this study. Jacob's words to his three eldest sons are not blessings in the ordinary sense but forfeitures, each rooted in an earlier deed the text has already recorded. Reuben, the firstborn, loses the standing his birth order would have given him because he defiled his father's bed with Bilhah (Genesis 35:22); the birthright itself later passes to Joseph's line (1 Chronicles 5:1–2). Simeon and Levi are rebuked together for the massacre at Shechem, which Jacob had condemned at the time it happened (Genesis 34:25–30), and their descendants are scattered rather than allotted a single territory of their own — a judgment Levi's later separation for priestly service (Numbers 18:20–24) softens without erasing.
Type & Shadow: The Lion of the Tribe of Judah
Judah's blessing, by contrast, is the one passage of this chapter that drew sustained attention in the corpus searched for this study. Brother Branham returned to verses 9–10 independently in at least two sermons, reading the promise that "the sceptre shall not depart from Judah…until Shiloh come" as pointing directly to Christ, and tying the lion imagery of verse 9 to the vision of Revelation 5, where the One announced as "the Lion of the tribe of Juda" turns out, when John looks, to be a slain Lamb.
“
Now, the gathering will be unto the Lord. As Jacob, in the 49th chapter, he said, “When Shiloh comes, and the gathering will be unto Him. There'll not be a lawgiver go until…from between the feet, until Shiloh come,” speaking of Judah, “and to Him shall the gathering be.”
— William Branham, “Questions and Answers” (62-0527, para. 138)
The following year, preaching on the seven seals, he returned to the same two verses to make the Lion/Lamb connection explicit:
“
Why out of Juda? “O Juda, a lawgiver shall not go before it, between his knees, until Shiloh comes. But He'll come through Juda.” And the Lion, the symbol of the tribe of Juda, has prevailed. He has overcome. And when he looked around to see where that Lion was, he saw a Lamb. Strange, look for a Lion and see a Lamb. The elder called Him a Lion. But when John looked, he saw a Lamb, “A Lamb as It had been slain from the foundation of the world.”
— William Branham, “The Breach Between the Seven Church Ages and the Seven Seals” (63-0317E, paras. 190–192)
Both readings treat "Shiloh" as a title for the Messiah rather than the town of the same name, in keeping with the long history of Jewish and Christian interpretation that sees this verse as the earliest explicit prophecy that the ruling line of Israel would run through Judah specifically — a line Matthew's genealogy and the writer of Hebrews both trace to Jesus (Matthew 1:1–3; Hebrews 7:14).

The Tribes Between

No sourced Branham commentary on verses 13–21 turned up in the corpus searched for this study, despite searches run against each tribe's distinctive imagery — Zebulun's harbor, Issachar's burden-bearing, Dan's serpent, Gad's reversal, Asher's plenty, and Naphtali's freedom. These seven blessings are read here plainly, on their own terms: brief, often cryptic, and largely geographic or characterological rather than doctrinal, describing the territory or temperament each tribe would come to occupy in the land rather than pointing forward typologically the way Judah's and Joseph's blessings do.

Joseph's Blessing

No sourced Branham commentary on verses 22–26 turned up in the corpus searched for this study, despite searches run against its most distinctive phrases, including "the shepherd, the stone of Israel" in verse 24. Joseph receives the longest and richest blessing of any son besides Judah's, described as a tree whose branches overhang a wall — fruitfulness that spills beyond its own boundary — and as an archer whose bow held steady under attack because his strength came from outside himself. The title "the stone of Israel" here anticipates later Scripture's language for a foundational, rejected stone (Psalm 118:22, quoted of Christ in 1 Peter 2:7), though Genesis itself does not develop the connection; it is left standing as an early instance of imagery Scripture returns to across both Testaments.

The Burial at Machpelah

No sourced Branham commentary on verses 27–33 turned up in the corpus searched for this study. Jacob's final instructions return, at the very end of his life, to the same insistence he had already made to Joseph in Genesis 47:29–31: he is not to be buried in Egypt. Here he gives the reason and the place by name — the cave Abraham purchased at Machpelah, already holding Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebekah, and Leah. Jacob's own burial there, carried out by Joseph in the chapter that follows, will complete a family plot spanning three generations and mark the last time any of the patriarchs is laid to rest in the land of promise until the Exodus generation returns.
