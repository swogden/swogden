Genesis

Chapter Nine

King James Version
And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth. 2And the fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon every beast of the earth, and upon every fowl of the air, upon all that moveth upon the earth, and upon all the fishes of the sea; into your hand are they delivered. 3Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you; even as the green herb have I given you all things. 4But flesh with the life thereof, which is the blood thereof, shall ye not eat.

5And surely your blood of your lives will I require; at the hand of every beast will I require it, and at the hand of man; at the hand of every man's brother will I require the life of man. 6Whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man. 7And you, be ye fruitful, and multiply; bring forth abundantly in the earth, and multiply therein.

8And God spake unto Noah, and to his sons with him, saying, 9And I, behold, I establish my covenant with you, and with your seed after you; 10And with every living creature that is with you, of the fowl, of the cattle, and of every beast of the earth with you; from all that go out of the ark, to every beast of the earth. 11And I will establish my covenant with you; neither shall all flesh be cut off any more by the waters of a flood; neither shall there any more be a flood to destroy the earth.

12And God said, This is the token of the covenant which I make between me and you and every living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations: 13I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth. 14And it shall come to pass, when I bring a cloud over the earth, that the bow shall be seen in the cloud: 15And I will remember my covenant, which is between me and you and every living creature of all flesh; and the waters shall no more become a flood to destroy all flesh. 16And the bow shall be in the cloud; and I will look upon it, that I may remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is upon the earth. 17And God said unto Noah, This is the token of the covenant, which I have established between me and all flesh that is upon the earth.

18And the sons of Noah, that went forth of the ark, were Shem, and Ham, and Japheth: and Ham is the father of Canaan. 19These are the three sons of Noah: and of them was the whole earth overspread.

20And Noah began to be an husbandman, and he planted a vineyard: 21And he drank of the wine, and was drunken; and he was uncovered within his tent. 22And Ham, the father of Canaan, saw the nakedness of his father, and told his two brethren without. 23And Shem and Japheth took a garment, and laid it upon both their shoulders, and went backward, and covered the nakedness of their father; and their faces were backward, and they saw not their father's nakedness.

24And Noah awoke from his wine, and knew what his younger son had done unto him. 25And he said, Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be unto his brethren. 26And he said, Blessed be the LORD God of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant. 27God shall enlarge Japheth, and he shall dwell in the tents of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant.

28And Noah lived after the flood three hundred and fifty years. 29And all the days of Noah were nine hundred and fifty years: and he died.

Commentary: A Bow Sworn by Oath, and a Second Fall in a Vineyard

A New Charge for a New World The Rainbow: A Covenant Sworn by Oath Seven Colors, Seven Ages Noah's Vineyard Cursed Be Canaan
With the ark emptied and the altar smoking, God gives the new world a new charge, and then, before the chapter closes, the man who was “perfect in his generations” falls in a way that has nothing to do with a flood. Genesis 9 holds both a covenant sealed forever and a family broken in a single afternoon.

A New Charge for a New World

Verses 1 through 7 repeat Eden's original blessing — be fruitful, multiply, replenish the earth — but add two things Eden never needed: permission to eat animal flesh, on the condition that its blood not be eaten, and the death penalty for murder, grounded explicitly in man still bearing God's image. Nothing in the corpus searched for this study directly comments on these verses, so this commentary states the text as it stands rather than assigning it a citation it does not have: a world now capable of the violence chapter 6 described is given both a wider provision and a firmer boundary than the world before the flood was given.

The Rainbow: A Covenant Sworn by Oath

Verses 8 through 17 give the flood's aftermath its most famous sign. Brother Branham treated the rainbow not as decoration but as the token of an oath — a covenant God made, in a sense, with Himself, since nothing exists greater than God to swear by.
“
“A covenant is always made by an oath…there was nobody greater than God, so He took an oath Himself, by Himself…this greenish…what does green represent? Life…God has promised, as He took a oath back there in Genesis, that He 'would no more' put the rainbow in the sky…that He 'would no more destroy this world by water.'”
— William Branham, “Revelation, Chapter Four, Part II” (61-0101, paras. 195, 197)
He read the covenant language of verses 12 through 17 as deliberately doubled — a promise to Noah personally, and a separate, larger oath God swears concerning the whole earth, tying it to the same kind of unbreakable, self-sworn promise given later to Abraham.
“
“In Exodus 23:13 and in Hebrews 6:12, God made a covenant with Himself and swore by Himself…There was no greater to swear by when He told Abraham and Isaac…that He would make a covenant with him, an everlasting covenant.”
— William Branham, “Revelation, Chapter Four, Part II” (61-0101, para. 194)

Seven Colors, Seven Ages

Brother Branham went further into the rainbow's own construction, finding a second layer of meaning in the number of colors it contains.
“
“Noah's rainbow composed primarily of seven colors…Now, if you notice, seven…seven-color rainbow. That meant seven bows! Seven bows, seven churches reflecting seven lights, each light wrapped into the other one.”
— William Branham, “Revelation, Chapter Four, Part II” (61-0101, paras. 176–177)
“
“Seven Spirits, seven colors, seven church ages, seven ministers, seven lights, everything's in a seven. God is perfect in 'seven.' God worked six days; seventh day, He rested. The world will exist six thousand years, and the seventh thousand is the Millennium.”
— William Branham, “Revelation, Chapter Four, Part II” (61-0101, para. 192)
Type & Shadow — A Bow That Cannot Be Broken
A bow is a weapon turned toward the one who draws it. Set in the clouds and pointed away from the earth, it is a picture of judgment permanently redirected — the same wrath that fell in chapter 7 now stands visibly disarmed every time the sign appears. What Noah receives here is not a suggestion or a hope, but an oath God swears on His own Name, which Brother Branham read as making it as unbreakable as anything God has ever promised.

Noah's Vineyard

Verses 20 and 21 turn without warning from covenant to catastrophe: the same man who walked with God through the greatest judgment in Scripture plants a vineyard, drinks its wine, and ends up drunk and exposed in his own tent. No search of the corpus for this study turned up a Branham quotation addressing this scene directly, so this commentary limits itself to what the text itself says rather than filling that gap with unsourced claims. What it says plainly is sobering on its own: surviving the flood did not make Noah immune to an ordinary, private failure, and Scripture records it without softening it.

Cursed Be Canaan

Verses 22 through 27 record the family's three different responses to what they found, and the consequences Noah pronounces once he wakes. The curse in verse 25 falls specifically on Canaan, Ham's son, not on Ham himself or on his other sons — a distinction the text itself makes and that this commentary preserves rather than broadening. Historically, Canaan's descendants became the Canaanite peoples who occupied the land Israel was later given, the very nations Joshua's conquest and the rest of Israel's history repeatedly confronts; verse 27's blessing on Japheth and Shem's tents runs forward into the family lines Genesis 10 lays out in the very next chapter. As with the two sections above, no sourced Branham commentary on this specific passage turned up in the corpus searched for this study, so nothing further is claimed here beyond what the text and its historical outworking in the rest of the Old Testament support.

Scripture Cross-References

Hebrews 6:13–18 — God, willing to show the immutability of His counsel, sware by Himself  •  Ezekiel 1:28 — as the appearance of the bow in the cloud, so was the appearance of the Lord  •  Revelation 4:3 — a rainbow round about the throne, in sight like unto an emerald  •  Leviticus 17:10–14 — the prohibition against eating blood, repeated under the Law  •  Exodus 20:13 — thou shalt not kill  •  Genesis 10:6–20 — the sons of Ham, including Canaan, and their nations  •  Joshua 9:23 — the Gibeonites, of the Canaanite peoples, made bondmen to Israel
