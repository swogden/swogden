Genesis

Chapter Thirteen

King James Version
And Abram went up out of Egypt, he, and his wife, and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the south. 2And Abram was very rich in cattle, in silver, and in gold. 3And he went on his journeys from the south even to Bethel, unto the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Hai; 4Unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first: and there Abram called on the name of the LORD.

5And Lot also, which went with Abram, had flocks, and herds, and tents. 6And the land was not able to bear them, that they might dwell together: for their substance was great, so that they could not dwell together. 7And there was a strife between the herdmen of Abram's cattle and the herdmen of Lot's cattle: and the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land.

8And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdmen and thy herdmen; for we be brethren. 9Is not the whole land before thee? separate thyself, I pray thee, from me: if thou wilt take the left hand, then I will go to the right; or if thou depart to the right hand, then I will go to the left.

10And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where, before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah, even as the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt, as thou comest unto Zoar. 11Then Lot chose him all the plain of Jordan; and Lot journeyed east: and they separated themselves the one from the other. 12Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom. 13But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly.

14And the LORD said unto Abram, after that Lot was separated from him, Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward: 15For all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever. 16And I will make thy seed as the dust of the earth: so that if a man can number the dust of the earth, then shall thy seed also be numbered. 17Arise, walk through the land in the length of it and in the breadth of it; for I will give it unto thee.

18Then Abram removed his tent, and came and dwelt in the plain of Mamre, which is in Hebron, and built there an altar unto the LORD.

Commentary: Two Men, Two Choices, Two Destinies

Returning to the Altar Strife and a Generous Offer Lot's Choice The Promise Renewed An Altar at Mamre
Genesis 13 brings Abram back from Egypt to the place he left, and then splits his household in two. What follows is one of the clearest side-by-side pictures in Genesis of two believers who received the same call and chose two different roads.

Returning to the Altar

Verses 1 through 4 bring Abram back out of Egypt to the exact place between Bethel and Hai where he had built his first altar in chapter 12. The text is precise about it — "unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first" — and there Abram calls on the name of the LORD again. Read against chapter 12's pattern of altars in Canaan and compromise in Egypt, this return is itself the point: failure in Egypt did not disqualify Abram, and his first act back in the land is worship at the very place he had worshipped before.

Strife and a Generous Offer

Verses 5 through 9 record the practical problem behind the chapter's real subject: too much livestock, not enough land, and conflict between the two households' herdsmen. Abram's response is to give away his own advantage — first choice of the land — rather than assert the rights that, as the elder and the one actually carrying God's promise, he could have claimed. Brother Branham read this whole scene from the pulpit as Scripture's own case study in total separation, opening an entire message from this chapter's account of Abram and Lot dividing.
“
“And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdsmen and thy herdsmen; for we are brethren…Is not the whole land before thee? separate thyself, I pray thee, from me.”
— William Branham, reading Genesis 13, “God's Word Calls for a Total Separation From Unbelief” (64-0121, para. 2)

Lot's Choice

Verses 10 through 13 give Lot the choice Abram offered, and Lot takes it on sight, without asking what kind of neighbors came with the view. The well-watered plain looks like Eden; the text immediately adds, almost as a warning label, that the men of Sodom were exceedingly wicked. Brother Branham treated Lot's choice as a picture of a believer who values what looks good over what is right, and Abraham as the pattern of the believer who holds to the Word regardless of what it costs him in the moment.
“
“Lot wanted the world. God…tore up his world, and separated Abraham and Lot. Lot, being a type, again, of a carnal believer who didn't think these things were true, he just went on down in Sodom. And he didn't have the real spunk to stand out…and call what was right 'right,' and wrong 'wrong,' so he went down.”
— William Branham, “God's Word Calls for a Total Separation From Unbelief” (64-0121, para. 74)
He put the contrast between the two men in even sharper terms elsewhere in the same message.
“
“Lot was just a penny, Abraham was a silver dollar, so it taken a hundred Lots to make an Abraham.”
— William Branham, “God's Word Calls for a Total Separation From Unbelief” (64-0121, para. 76)
Type & Shadow — Two Believers, One Separation
Brother Branham read the parting of Abraham and Lot as more than one family argument settled peacefully; he treated it as a recurring pattern God repeats whenever He is about to act — a carnal, world-loving believer separated out from the one who holds to the promise. He connected this same pattern forward to the days just before Sodom's destruction in Genesis 19, and from there to Jesus' own words that His coming would be "as it was in the days of Sodom" — the same kind of separation between the carnal and the genuine happening again before the end.

The Promise Renewed

Verses 14 through 17 record God speaking to Abram immediately after Lot leaves — not before, the text is careful to note, but "after that Lot was separated from him." The promise is repeated and enlarged: all the land in every direction, and a seed like the dust of the earth. Read alongside the type just discussed, the timing is worth noticing on its own terms: the fuller promise comes once the mixed household becomes a separated one, though this commentary does not go further than what the text itself makes plain, since no sourced Branham commentary on this specific timing turned up in the corpus searched for this study.

An Altar at Mamre

The chapter closes with Abram moving to the plain of Mamre in Hebron and building yet another altar there. No corpus quotation addressing this specific altar turned up for this study, so this commentary simply notes the pattern it completes: three altars now stand behind Abram — at Shechem, at Bethel, and now at Hebron — each one marking a place where he chose worship over the world's version of the same ground Lot had just chosen for its view.

Scripture Cross-References
