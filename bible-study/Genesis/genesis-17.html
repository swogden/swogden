Genesis

Chapter Seventeen

King James Version
And when Abram was ninety years old and nine, the LORD appeared to Abram, and said unto him, I am the Almighty God; walk before me, and be thou perfect. 2And I will make my covenant between me and thee, and will multiply thee exceedingly. 3And Abram fell on his face: and God talked with him, saying, 4As for me, behold, my covenant is with thee, and thou shalt be a father of many nations. 5Neither shall thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name shall be Abraham; for a father of many nations have I made thee. 6And I will make thee exceeding fruitful, and I will make nations of thee, and kings shall come out of thee.

7And I will establish my covenant between me and thee and thy seed after thee in their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee. 8And I will give unto thee, and to thy seed after thee, the land wherein thou art a stranger, all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession; and I will be their God.

9And God said unto Abraham, Thou shalt keep my covenant therefore, thou, and thy seed after thee in their generations. 10This is my covenant, which ye shall keep, between me and you and thy seed after thee; Every man child among you shall be circumcised. 11And ye shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskin; and it shall be a token of the covenant betwixt me and you. 12And he that is eight days old shall be circumcised among you, every man child in your generations, he that is born in the house, or bought with money of any stranger, which is not of thy seed. 13He that is born in thy house, and he that is bought with thy money, must needs be circumcised: and my covenant shall be in your flesh for an everlasting covenant. 14And the uncircumcised man child whose flesh of his foreskin is not circumcised, that soul shall be cut off from his people; he hath broken my covenant.

15And God said unto Abraham, As for Sarai thy wife, thou shalt not call her name Sarai, but Sarah shall her name be. 16And I will bless her, and give thee a son also of her: yea, I will bless her, and she shall be a mother of nations; kings of people shall be of her. 17Then Abraham fell upon his face, and laughed, and said in his heart, Shall a child be born unto him that is an hundred years old? and shall Sarah, that is ninety years old, bear? 18And Abraham said unto God, O that Ishmael might live before thee!

19And God said, Sarah thy wife shall bear thee a son indeed; and thou shalt call his name Isaac: and I will establish my covenant with him for an everlasting covenant, and with his seed after him. 20And as for Ishmael, I have heard thee: Behold, I have blessed him, and will make him fruitful, and will multiply him exceedingly; twelve princes shall he beget, and I will make him a great nation. 21But my covenant will I establish with Isaac, which Sarah shall bear unto thee at this set time in the next year. 22And he left off talking with him, and God went up from Abraham.

23And Abraham took Ishmael his son, and all that were born in his house, and all that were bought with his money, every male among the men of Abraham's house; and circumcised the flesh of their foreskin in the selfsame day, as God had said unto him. 24And Abraham was ninety years old and nine, when he was circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin. 25And Ishmael his son was thirteen years old, when he was circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin. 26In the selfsame day was Abraham circumcised, and Ishmael his son. 27And all the men of his house, born in the house, and bought with money of the stranger, were circumcised with him.

Commentary: New Names, and a Sign Cut in the Flesh

I Am the Almighty God A New Name, Part of God's Own The Token in the Flesh Sarah, and the Laughter of Faith Ishmael Blessed, Isaac Chosen
Thirteen years pass silently between chapters 16 and 17 — Ishmael's entire childhood — before God speaks to Abram again. When He does, He renames everyone involved, institutes a permanent physical sign, and names the promised son before he is even conceived.

I Am the Almighty God

Verses 1 and 2 open with a new title for God Himself: El Shaddai, "the Almighty." Brother Branham traced the Hebrew behind this name to a root meaning "the breast," the source of a mother's nourishment, and read the title as God's promise to sustain and provide for Abraham out of His own strength rather than Abraham's.
“
“The word in the Hebrew is from the word 'El Shaddai,' which means 'the breast, the nourishing,' like the woman nourishes her baby…'I am El Shaddai. I am the breast. I'm the Almighty.' In other words, 'No matter how weak you are, I can nourish you back to health again.'”
— William Branham, “At Thy Word, Lord” (48-0305, para. 22)

A New Name, Part of God's Own

Verses 3 through 8 record the covenant's terms and, with them, Abram's new name. Brother Branham pointed out that the change was not cosmetic — the syllable added to "Abram" to make "Abraham" was, in his reading, part of God's own covenant name.
“
“When God, before He made Abraham the father of nations, He changed his name from Abram to Abraham…He is going to bring through him the father of nations. He took part of His Own Name, Elohim, and made him Abraham.”
— William Branham, “Jehovah-Jireh” (61-0312, para. 192)

The Token in the Flesh

Verses 9 through 14 institute circumcision as the covenant's permanent, physical sign, performed on the eighth day of a child's life and required of every male in Abraham's household, servants included. Brother Branham was careful, in discussing this sign elsewhere in Abraham's story, to keep its order straight: the sign followed faith, it did not produce it.
“
“Abraham believed God, that's right, and it was imputed to him for righteousness. But God give him a sign; circumcised him, as a sign, a seal.”
— William Branham, “He Swore by Himself” (54-1212, para. 122)
He extended the sign's meaning to the New Testament believer, treating physical circumcision itself as a shadow of an inward reality.
“
“Every son of Abraham is circumcised today, not of the flesh, and of the heart. The Holy Spirit circumcises the heart, cuts away the things of the world, and makes you a new creature in Christ Jesus. Circumcision is 'cut off surplus flesh.'”
— William Branham, “He Swore by Himself” (54-1212, para. 123)
Type & Shadow — A Sign That Cannot Save
Genesis 15 recorded Abraham's faith counted for righteousness; Genesis 17, roughly a decade and a half later, adds circumcision as the visible mark of a covenant already in force. The order matters: righteousness came by belief alone, and the sign was added afterward to seal what faith had already secured. Paul makes this exact sequence the center of his own argument in Romans 4 — that Abraham is the father of all who believe, circumcised or not, because he was declared righteous while still uncircumcised.

Sarah, and the Laughter of Faith

Verses 15 through 19 turn to Sarai, renamed Sarah, and to Abraham's own reaction when told that this ninety-year-old woman would bear the promised son: he falls on his face and laughs, then asks God to let Ishmael be the one to carry the blessing instead. God's answer names the child before he exists — Isaac, meaning laughter — and repeats that the everlasting covenant runs through him specifically.
“
“When Abraham's boy Isaac was born, of course, he called him Isaac, which was the promised one. God had promised Abraham, had changed his name from Abram to Abraham. He was going to make him a father of nations.”
— William Branham, “Jehovah-Jireh” (55-0817, para. 13)

Ishmael Blessed, Isaac Chosen

Verses 20 and 21 close the vision with a distinction the text makes carefully: Ishmael is genuinely blessed, promised twelve princes and a great nation of his own, but the covenant itself — the everlasting one carrying the promise given in chapter 15 — is established with Isaac, not with him. Real blessing and covenant inheritance are not the same thing in this chapter, a distinction already anticipated by chapter 16's account of Ishmael as the child born of impatience rather than promise.
Verses 22 through 27 record Abraham's response once the vision ends: immediate, complete obedience, circumcising every male in his household that same day, Ishmael included, though Ishmael was already thirteen years old. No sourced Branham commentary on this specific closing scene of household-wide obedience turned up in the corpus searched for this study; the text itself is plain enough that this commentary lets it stand without embellishment.

Scripture Cross-References

Romans 4:9–12 — Abraham's faith counted for righteousness before he was circumcised  •  Colossians 2:11–12 — circumcision made without hands, in the putting off of the sins of the flesh  •  Luke 1:59–60 — the eighth-day circumcision and naming still practiced in the New Testament  •  Genesis 21:1–7 — the birth of Isaac, fulfilling the promise given here  •  Galatians 3:16 — the seed to whom the promises were made, which is Christ  •  Genesis 25:12–16 — the twelve princes of Ishmael's line, named in full
