Genesis

Chapter Twenty-Two

King James Version
And it came to pass after these things, that God did tempt Abraham, and said unto him, Abraham: and he said, Behold, here I am. 2And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.

3And Abraham rose up early in the morning, and saddled his ass, and took two of his young men with him, and Isaac his son, and clave the wood for the burnt offering, and rose up, and went unto the place of which God had told him. 4Then on the third day Abraham lifted up his eyes, and saw the place afar off. 5And Abraham said unto his young men, Abide ye here with the ass; and I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you. 6And Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering, and laid it upon Isaac his son; and he took the fire in his hand, and a knife; and they went both of them together. 7And Isaac spake unto Abraham his father, and said, My father: and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering? 8And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.

9And they came to the place which God had told him of; and Abraham built an altar there, and laid the wood in order, and bound Isaac his son, and laid him on the altar upon the wood. 10And Abraham stretched forth his hand, and took the knife to slay his son. 11And the angel of the LORD called unto him out of heaven, and said, Abraham, Abraham: and he said, Here am I. 12And he said, Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.

13And Abraham lifted up his eyes, and looked, and behold behind him a ram caught in a thicket by his horns: and Abraham went and took the ram, and offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son. 14And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovahjireh: as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.

15And the angel of the LORD called unto Abraham out of heaven the second time, 16And said, By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son: 17That in blessing I will bless thee, and in multiplying I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven, and as the sand which is upon the sea shore; and thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies; 18And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice. 19So Abraham returned unto his young men, and they rose up and went together to Beersheba; and Abraham dwelt at Beersheba.

20And it came to pass after these things, that it was told Abraham, saying, Behold, Milcah, she hath also born children unto thy brother Nahor; 21Huz his firstborn, and Buz his brother, and Kemuel the father of Aram, 22And Chesed, and Hazo, and Pildash, and Jidlaph, and Bethuel. 23And Bethuel begat Rebekah: these eight Milcah did bear to Nahor, Abraham's brother. 24And his concubine, whose name was Reumah, she bare also Tebah, and Gaham, and Thahash, and Maachah.

Commentary: Jehovah-Jireh, the Lord Will Provide

Take Now Thy Son The Wood Upon Isaac's Shoulder Obedience Unto the Knife Jehovah-Jireh: The Ram in the Thicket Sworn by Himself, the Second Time
Of every chapter studied so far in Genesis, none drew more return visits from Brother Branham than this one. He preached entire sermons titled simply "Jehovah-Jireh," and this scene — the son, the wood, the knife, the ram — supplied him one of his most frequently repeated pictures of Christ's own sacrifice.

Take Now Thy Son

Verses 1 and 2 open with the test itself, coming years after Isaac's birth and long after Abraham had staked his whole testimony, publicly, on this one promised son. Brother Branham read Abraham's willingness to obey as resting entirely on a settled conviction: if God required Isaac's death, God could also raise him back to life, because the promise still stood.
“
“Abraham said, 'Lord, if You get…Back yonder when I was barren, I had nothing. I was a sinner, and You made me something. You made me a promise. And here it is, You give me this boy…Now You told me to offer him up, to kill him. And You told me, that, through me, I would be the father of many nations.'”
— William Branham, “The Uncertain Sound” (60-1218, para. 332)
He pointed directly to the New Testament's own explanation of Abraham's reasoning, reading the account through the lens of Hebrews 11.
“
“By faith Abraham, when he was tried, offered up Isaac…of whom it was said, that in Isaac shall thy seed be called: accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead; from whence also he received him in a figure.”
— William Branham, quoting Hebrews 11:17–19, “The Uncertain Sound” (60-1218, para. 331)

The Wood Upon Isaac's Shoulder

Verses 3 through 8 follow the three-day journey and the moment Isaac himself asks the obvious question: the fire and the wood are ready, but where is the lamb? Brother Branham read the boy carrying the wood up the mountain as a direct picture of Christ carrying His own cross to Golgotha.
“
“He put the wood upon Isaac's shoulder, a type of the true Seed, Jesus, to make us Abraham's Seed, took the wood for the sacrifice to lay on, upon His Own shoulders, when He climbed up Golgotha.”
— William Branham, “Jehovah-Jireh” (61-0312, para. 222)
He treated Abraham's answer to Isaac's question — "God will provide Himself a lamb" — as the whole of Abraham's faith compressed into a single sentence: no explanation, only trust that obedience to the instruction was his part, and provision was God's.
“
“He said, 'My son, God will provide for Himself a lamb for this sacrifice.' There you are. 'Only thing I'm doing is just following instructions, it's up to God to provide the lamb.'”
— William Branham, “Abraham's Covenant Confirmed” (61-0318, para. 153)

Obedience Unto the Knife

Verses 9 and 10 bring the scene to its point of no return: the altar built, Isaac bound and laid upon the wood, the knife raised. Brother Branham dwelt on Isaac's own willingness once he understood what was happening, reading the son's submission as itself a type of Christ's obedience even to death.
“
“Then he said, 'Isaac, put your hands behind you.' He took a string and begin to tie his hands, Isaac knew then. Watch his obedience, like Jesus the true Son of God, obedience unto death, took the cross. Tied his hands and his feet, Abraham lifted up the little boy and laid him up on the rock.”
— William Branham, “Abraham's Covenant Confirmed” (61-0318, para. 155)

Jehovah-Jireh: The Ram in the Thicket

Verses 11 through 14 turn the entire scene: the Angel stops Abraham's hand at the last possible moment, and a ram appears, caught by its horns, to die in Isaac's place. Brother Branham returned again and again to one detail as proof this was no coincidence — the location itself made the ram's presence impossible by any natural explanation.
“
“About that time, he heard something behind him. Looked, and there was a ram hooked in the bushes. Where did that ram come from? He was a hundred miles from civilization, back in the wilderness where there's lions, wolves, jackals, and all those things like that, that kills sheep. And then he was way up on top the mountain, where there's no water or nothing else up there for the sheep to live on. And it wasn't there, a minute before that. Looked back, and there it was, the next minute, there. Jehovah-jireh!”
— William Branham, “The Uncertain Sound” (60-1218, paras. 335–336)
He named this as the first of God's seven compound redemptive names revealed in Scripture, and read the ram's substitution as the earliest full picture of the atonement in the Bible.
“
“You know Who that ram was? That was Christ; offered Him up, instead. And Abraham called the name of the place Jehovah-jireh: as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen. Jehovah-jireh, which was God's first redemptive Name. Jehovah-jireh means, 'the Lord will provide a sacrifice.'”
— William Branham, “Believing God” (52-0224, paras. 122–123)
He was careful, too, to distinguish what happened from a mere vision, insisting the text records a real, physical death in Isaac's place.
“
“Now, that wasn't a vision Abraham saw, because the ram bled, and a vision don't bleed. See? He killed the ram. God spoke it into existence, and Abraham took it out of existence in less than a minute's time…because He had brought somebody to that place, testing him. The Bible said Abraham was tested, and the Seed of Abraham is tested.”
— William Branham, “Abraham's Covenant Confirmed” (61-0318, para. 163)
Type & Shadow — The Father, the Son, and the Provided Lamb
Brother Branham read this entire chapter as a single, continuous picture of Calvary set two thousand years in advance: a father willing to give his only, long-promised son; the son himself carrying the wood for his own sacrifice up the mountain, submitting without resistance once he understood; and, at the last moment, a substitute provided by God Himself so that the son who carried the wood would not be the one who died. The location's later name, Jehovah-Jireh, and its identification elsewhere in Scripture with the region of Jerusalem, only deepened the type in his preaching — the same ground where, centuries later, the greater Substitute would actually die.

Sworn by Himself, the Second Time

Verses 15 through 19 record the Angel's second call from heaven and God's oath, sworn on Himself since there was nothing greater to swear by, renewing and expanding the covenant blessing because Abraham had not withheld his son. Brother Branham read this second oath as the permanent seal on Abraham's testing — proof that when God brings a believer to that same final point of testing, standing alone with nothing left to lean on but the Word itself, it is never arbitrary, but always leads to a confirmed and expanded promise.
“
“God looked down and said, 'His Seed shall possess the gates. I've sworn by Myself that I'll do these things.' There's no one greater He could swear by, but He swore by Himself. Then, if He let Abraham be tested to that final point, He's got to test you and me for that final moment, that time of decision when everything is away from you. You have to stand alone there.”
— William Branham, “Possessing the Enemy's Gates” (59-1108, para. 33)
Verses 20 through 24 close the chapter with a brief report of Nahor's family back in Mesopotamia, naming Bethuel and, through him, Rebekah — the woman who will become Isaac's wife in chapter 24. No sourced commentary on this genealogical notice itself turned up in the corpus searched for this study; its purpose becomes clear only once Rebekah enters the narrative directly.
